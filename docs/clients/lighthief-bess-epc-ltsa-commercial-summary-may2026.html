Purpose. This note summarises the main commercial, payment, security, title, defects, delay remedy, warranty, liability, and insurance provisions you would typically see in a turnkey BESS supply and build agreement with Lighthief Cyprus Ltd, together with a long-term operations and maintenance / warranty services agreement. Your signed agreements always prevail. Figures and optional mechanisms apply only as set out in your offer letter and executed contracts.

This is a summary for discussion. It is not an offer, not legal advice, and not a substitute for your own counsel review of the draft EPC, LTSA, and equipment supplier undertakings issued for your project.

1. What you are contracting for

  • EPC agreement — engineering, procurement, construction, commissioning, and handover of the battery energy storage system, with Lighthief as your contracting counterparty.
  • Long-term service agreement (LTSA) — ongoing services, monitoring support, and extended performance commitments for the operating period, as scoped in your package.
  • Equipment manufacturer undertakings — where included, a direct warranty undertaking from the battery equipment manufacturer in favour of the project, alongside Lighthief’s EPC obligations.

2. Single point of responsibility

Lighthief remains responsible to you for delivery, quality, and performance of the system as a whole, including equipment sourced from the manufacturer. Subcontracting or manufacturer supply does not reduce Lighthief’s obligations under the EPC.

3. Contract price structure

The contract price is usually split into an equipment supply portion and an engineering and construction services portion (in the EPC these may be labelled as separate components). Milestone percentages below apply to the total contract price unless your schedule states otherwise.

Where a confirmed price or indexation mechanism is agreed for your deal, any caps, walk-away rights, and effective dates are set only in your executed contract and price schedule.

5. Financial security

Advance payment guarantee

Where the EPC requires it, an advance payment guarantee is obtained (typically from the equipment manufacturer’s bank) for an amount reflecting the advance and pre-shipment instalments that relate to equipment supply, in line with your payment schedule. The guarantee is usually valid until equipment is delivered to site plus an agreed short tail period. Instalments relating purely to on-site services may fall outside that guarantee — your schedule will show the split.

Performance bond

A performance bond (commonly 5% of the equipment supply portion of the price) typically runs until the end of the defects liability period after PAC.

6. Title, risk, and lender arrangements

  • Risk of loss usually passes when equipment is delivered to your site and unloaded, as defined in the EPC.
  • Title usually passes when provisional acceptance is achieved and the PAC instalment is paid. Retention of the 5% does not normally delay title.
  • After title passes, you can normally grant security to a project lender over the equipment, and assign rights under the EPC for financing purposes, within the clauses agreed in your version of the contract.

7. Defects liability period

Under the current Lighthief EPC template, the defects liability period after PAC is three months. There is no separate long “final acceptance” retention gate; retention is released after that period subject to punch-list rules in the contract.

8. Delay remedies (liquidated damages)

If the contractor is responsible for delay beyond agreed dates, the EPC normally provides for liquidated damages calculated on a defined fee base, with tiered daily rates and an overall cap (commonly expressed as a percentage of the contract price per site). Excused delays (for example grid or authority issues, force majeure, or matters defined in your EPC) are excluded.

Where your contract includes separate bases for equipment delivery and commissioning delays, the applicable base for each phase is set in the executed EPC.

9. Warranty and long-term performance

  • EPC warranty — covers defects in workmanship and supplied equipment for the warranty period, with performance metrics for the battery system (state of health, efficiency, cycle count, etc.) as specified in your technical schedule and warranty article.
  • Coastal / harsh environment — shorter warranty periods may apply to certain outdoor equipment very close to the sea, as stated in the EPC.
  • LTSA — availability and extended-year performance commitments (for example annual availability thresholds and service-fee adjustments if they are not met) are governed by the LTSA, not the EPC.

10. Limitation of liability

The EPC normally caps the contractor’s aggregate liability in tiered bands as a percentage of the contract price (for example a lower cap for warranty-type claims, a higher cap for other breaches, and uncapped treatment for fraud, wilful misconduct, and certain manufacturing defect scenarios). Carve-outs for personal injury and similar matters are also standard. Your executed contract contains the exact percentages and definitions.

11. Insurance (indicative minimums)

Under the template EPC, Lighthief is typically required to maintain contractor’s all risks on the works, public liability, and professional indemnity at minimum limit levels stated in the agreement (commonly €5 million public liability and €2 million professional indemnity per occurrence, subject to your final contract). Evidence of cover is normally delivered shortly after the first payment becomes due.
