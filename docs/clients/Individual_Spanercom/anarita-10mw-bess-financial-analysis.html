Why BESS for a 10 MW PV Park? Cyprus's electricity grid cannot absorb all solar generation during midday hours, resulting in mandatory curtailment — energy the park produces but cannot sell. A co-located BESS captures this otherwise-lost energy, stores it, and discharges during peak evening hours when prices are highest. The cost of charging is effectively €0/MWh since the energy would otherwise be curtailed (wasted).

7. Recommendation

Optimal Size Recommendation

Decision Framework

Scenario Curtailment Range Best Option ROI Range Payback Rationale
Conservative 45–50% Option A (20 MWh) 34.7% 2.9 yr Lowest capex, highest ROI. BESS fully utilized every day.
Expected 55–65% Option B (30 MWh) 33.2–38.2% 2.6–3.0 yr Best balance. Captures more revenue as curtailment rises.
Aggressive 70%+ Option C (40 MWh) 33.9%+ 3.0 yr Maximum capture. Highest absolute revenue at extreme curtailment.
Our Recommendation: Option B — 10 MW / 30 MWh (€4,040,500)

Option A delivers the best ROI at 34.7%, but its 20 MWh capacity is already fully utilized at the park's current 48% curtailment — meaning it cannot capture additional revenue as curtailment increases. Option B starts with a strong 33.2% ROI at 55% curtailment and improves to 38.2% at 60%, reaching 39.3% at 70%, capturing up to €535K more annual revenue than Option A. With Anarita's curtailment already hitting 66–67% in spring 2025 and averaging 48% for the year, the additional €1M investment in Option B is recovered in under 2 years from the incremental revenue alone.

Why Not Option A?

Ceiling Risk Option A's 20 MWh battery is "full" every day at the park's current 48% curtailment. With curtailment already reaching 66–67% in spring 2025, Option A leaves up to €535K/yr of recoverable revenue on the table — energy that is curtailed but the battery is too small to capture. Over 15 years, this represents €6–8M in lost revenue.

Why Not Option C?

Underutilization at Current Levels Option C's 40 MWh battery requires 70%+ curtailment to approach full utilization. At 45% curtailment, it delivers 19.4% ROI with a 5.2-year payback — the additional 10 MWh over Option B is rarely filled. It becomes attractive only if curtailment structurally exceeds 70%, which is possible but not the base case.

9. Future Revenue

Future Revenue Opportunities

Important: Not Included in Base Financial Model The following revenue streams are not included in the financial projections above. They represent additional upside that could materially increase returns once Cyprus opens ancillary services and grid-balancing markets to BESS operators.

Frequency Containment Reserve (FCR)

€200K – €600K/yr

Rate: €10–15/MW/hr

Hours: 2,000–4,000 hrs/yr

Calculation: 10 MW × €10–15/MW/hr × 2,000–4,000 hrs

Primary frequency response — fast injection/absorption to stabilise grid frequency. Cyprus TSO is developing this market as part of EU harmonisation.

Automatic Frequency Restoration (aFRR)

€50K – €300K/yr

Rate: €5–10/MW/hr

Hours: 1,000–3,000 hrs/yr

Calculation: 10 MW × €5–10/MW/hr × 1,000–3,000 hrs

Secondary frequency response — automated restoration of frequency deviations. Expected to launch on Cyprus grid by 2027–2028.

Grid Arbitrage (DAM Trading)

Up to €516K/yr

Spread: €81.86/MWh (peak – midday)

Net after RTE: €70.66/MWh

Calculation: €70.66 × 20 MWh × 365 days (1 cycle/day)

Buy cheap midday, sell at evening peak. Only available on non-curtailment days — curtailment recovery is always more profitable (€157.96 vs €70.66 per MWh stored).

Combined Upside Potential

€250K – €1.4M/yr

On top of curtailment recovery revenue

Priority order:

1. Curtailment recovery (always first)

2. FCR (stackable, doesn't require cycling)

3. aFRR (partially stackable)

4. Grid arbitrage (non-curtailment days only)

Market Timing

Cyprus is an EU member state and must harmonise its electricity market rules with the EU Clean Energy Package. This includes opening ancillary services (FCR, aFRR, mFRR) to storage assets. The TSO (TSOC) has indicated a phased rollout beginning 2027. BESS assets installed now will be positioned to capture these markets from day one. As of February 2026, BESS systems cannot buy from the DAM grid — all revenue in the base model is curtailment recovery only.

10. Levelized Cost of Storage

Levelized Cost of Storage (LCOS)

LCOS measures the total cost per MWh of energy delivered over the system lifetime. It includes capital expenditure, O&M, and extended warranty costs across 15 years, divided by total energy discharged (accounting for SOH degradation and round-trip efficiency).
