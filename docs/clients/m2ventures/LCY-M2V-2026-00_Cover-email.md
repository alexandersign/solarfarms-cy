**Document 1 — the base case.** One megawatt-peak of solar with a one megawatt / four megawatt-hour battery, on 1,471 × 680 Wp bifacial modules. Everything else in the pack is this block, scaled. South-facing yield is no longer a design assumption: it is 2,194 kWh/kWp, taken from JRC PVGIS 5.3 at our Sotira TMY (south-facing optimum 31°, front 2,006) plus the same bifacial rear-side model we ran for Sotira (+9.3% at albedo 0.25). It is worth ten minutes before the project pages, because it contains the one point on which we would ask you to revisit your brief.

Your inquiry specifies up to 5 MWp of solar with up to 5 MWh of storage. In Cyprus that ratio does not work. The island has no interconnector, and in the first five months of 2026 the grid refused roughly 65% of potential solar generation — up from 47% across 2025 and 29% in 2024. A one-hour battery on a 1 MWp plant captures under a quarter of what the grid throws away. A four-hour battery captures 85%, and the fourth megawatt-hour alone earns a 35% return on its own cost. The economics of a Cypriot solar asset in 2026 are not really solar economics at all; they are arbitrage between a midday hour where power is refused outright and an evening peak that clears at some of the highest prices in Europe, and the size of the battery is what determines how much of that spread you can reach.

The practical consequence is that a 5 MWp portfolio built the way we would build it carries approximately 20 MWh of storage, not 5 MWh. That is a materially larger capital commitment than your brief contemplates, and it is better raised now than at term sheet.
