## What is curtailment?

On busy sunny days, the electricity network can become overloaded. EAC (the grid operator) may send a signal to commercial solar sites to **stop exporting** power for a period.

Many basic installations respond by **switching solar off completely**. That means you lose free daytime power for your offices, machinery, cooling, or other loads — and you buy more from the grid.

**Curtailment is normal.** How your system reacts is what matters.

## How it works (simple)

1. **We measure** power at your main connection to the grid.  
2. **When EAC signals curtailment**, the inverter automatically stops export.  
3. **Solar powers your business** — not the grid — during that time.  
4. **When the signal ends**, normal operation returns (including export if you have surplus and net billing).

---

## Your electricity bill

On **net billing**, you will not receive export credits during curtailed hours — because you are not exporting. You **will** still reduce what you buy from the grid by using solar on site. For most commercial clients, **that on-site saving is the real value** of this protection.

Do not bypass EAC’s curtailment device. Our approach is the approved way to protect your savings.
