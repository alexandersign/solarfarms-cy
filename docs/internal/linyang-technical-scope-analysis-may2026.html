3. Shared / Interface Zone — Supervision vs. Execution

The most contractually sensitive zone is the interface between Linyang's installation supervision and Lighthief's physical execution. The Sales Contract 17 March explicitly shifted PCS-to-BESS connection to Buyer scope (§5A(g)). This means Lighthief's team physically installs the critical DC/AC interconnections — but Linyang supervises and signs off. If a defect arises from this interface, determining fault will require analysis of Linyang's sign-off documentation.

However, Linyang's monthly maintenance requirements (BMS data read, log save, software update check, cable inspection) go beyond what two annual visits can cover. To maintain Linyang warranty, clients on Tier C must accept that some monthly physical items are advisory (visual inspection by client-designated person) rather than SP-attended. Alternatively, offer a monthly site visit add-on at additional cost.

4.2 Critical Battery Operating Conditions (Warranty Voiding Triggers)

These are defined in both the User Manual and Warranty Terms v2, and incorporated into the Sales Contract §8D.2 and EPC v5.0 §10.5:

ConditionThresholdDurationConsequenceEPC Protection
Cell deep discharge (voltage) Discharge cell voltage <2.8V 120 consecutive hours Warranty null and void for affected cells and modules EPC §10.5(a) — carve-out for grid fault, EMS malfunction, FM. SOC monitoring alert obligation.
Cluster SOC at zero Any cluster SOC = 0% 120 consecutive hours Warranty null and void EPC §10.5(b) — same carve-outs apply.
Extreme undervoltage Discharge cell voltage ≤2.5V Any single occurrence Warranty null and void — immediate EPC §10.5(c) — same carve-outs. Note: this is the most severe condition — a single event voids warranty.
Low SOC prolonged SOC <5% 7 consecutive days Battery warranty void for affected cells/modules EPC §10.5(d) — same carve-outs. Added per EPC v3 amendment. LTSA 24/7 monitoring triggers alert at SOC <10% within 24 hours.
LTSA monitoring obligation is critical here: EPC §10.5 carve-out states warranty is NOT voided if the condition was caused by "failure by the Contractor to provide a monitoring alert under an LTSA with 24/7 monitoring (Tier A+), where such alert would have enabled the Client to prevent the condition." This means if Lighthief has a Tier A/C LTSA active and fails to alert the client when SOC drops below 10%, and the SOC subsequently drops below 5% for 7 days, Lighthief is liable for the voided warranty costs — even though the client caused the SOC issue by not responding. The 24-hour SOC <10% alert in LTSA §10.5 is therefore not just good service — it is Lighthief's contractual protection mechanism.

5. RTE — Technical Clarification of Warranted Values

5.1 RTE Calculation Breakdown (from Linyang RTE Calculation doc, Feb 2025)

The full system AC-AC RTE of 86.32% used in contracts is calculated as follows (with auxiliary power consumption included):

RTE (without auxiliary) = 87.8%, calculated as: 99.95% (HV transformer) × 99.95% (MV cable) × 99.00% (MV transformer) × 99.95% (LV cable) × 98.20% (PCS rectifying) × 99.80% (DC cable) × 96.50% (Battery charging) × 97.50% (Battery discharging) × 99.80% (DC cable) × 98.50% (PCS inverting) × 99.95% (LV cable) × 99.00% (MV transformer) × 99.50% (MV cable) × 99.50% (HV transformer) ≈ 87.8% (at MV side, without auxiliary power) Auxiliary power consumption: ~1.48% additional loss Full system AC-AC RTE (at POC, with aux): ≈ 86.32% PAC field test threshold: 84.0% (2.32 percentage point below warranted 86.32%) This tolerance accounts for: site conditions, test instrument accuracy, ambient temperature variation

6. Technical Discrepancies Found — Contract Implications

T1 — Arbitration Venue Conflict: Warranty Terms v2 (SHIAC Shanghai) vs. Sales Contract (SIAC Singapore)

Rating: HIGH
Finding: Linyang's Warranty Terms v2 (Section XIII) specifies Shanghai International Arbitration Centre (SHIAC) as the dispute resolution forum. The Sales Contract uses SIAC Singapore. The OEM Direct Warranty Undertaking uses SIAC Singapore.

Impact: If a warranty claim is disputed and Linyang invokes Warranty Terms v2, they may argue SHIAC jurisdiction. If Lighthief or a client relies on the Sales Contract or OEM DWU, they would go to SIAC. This could result in parallel proceedings or jurisdictional challenges.

Mitigation: Sales Contract §1A.1 establishes the Sales Contract governs in case of conflict — and the Sales Contract specifies SIAC. The Sales Contract, once signed, supersedes Warranty Terms v2 as the contractual framework. However, add a clause to Sales Contract confirmation: "In the event of conflict between this Contract and Linyang's standard Warranty Terms (LYCN/WI-3410), this Contract shall prevail." This is implicit in current §1A.1 language but should be explicit for warranty disputes.

T2 — Fire Suppression Agent (Perfluoroketone / FK5112) — AXA PFAS Exclusion

Rating: HIGH — Insurance Gap Confirmed
Finding: The Power Atlantic 5MWh container uses FK5112 Perfluoroketone (PFK) fire suppression, also identified in the User Manual as "perfluo" agent. This is a PFAS-family compound (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances). The AXA Tianping CGL policy (received 9 Mar 2026) contains a PFAS exclusion listed in the RFP analysis (Section 8.4).

Impact: If a fire suppression system activates and discharges FK5112 compound, and this causes: (a) soil/groundwater contamination; (b) third-party exposure claims; (c) environmental regulatory penalties — AXA will likely invoke the PFAS exclusion and decline coverage.

Mitigation: (1) Confirm with Marsh whether the Lighthief CAR/environmental liability policy can include a PFAS sublimit or PFAS carve-in for fire suppression agent discharge. (2) Check whether Linyang can provide confirmation that FK5112 is classified as a short-chain PFAS (which have different regulatory status to long-chain PFAS in some jurisdictions). (3) Include in Lighthief's environmental liability RFP cover letter that the fire suppression agent is FK5112/perfluoroketone and request explicit cover.

T3 — "Only Linyang-Approved Personnel Can Maintain Battery Internals"

Rating: MEDIUM — Operational and Warranty Impact
Finding: User Manual Section 1.6 states: "There are no user-maintainable parts inside the battery unit. Only personnel approved by LINYANG can remove, replace and dispose of the batteries. Users are not allowed to maintain batteries without guidance."

Impact: Lighthief's 6 field engineers cannot open battery containers for internal repairs without Linyang authorization. This means: (a) any internal battery fault requires calling Linyang service personnel; (b) Lighthief's field team handles external maintenance only (cables, cooling system external, fire system sensors, BMS communication, container enclosure); (c) if Linyang's response time is slow (Distribution §14.2: critical issues 4-hour remote, 5 Business Days on-site), there may be extended downtime during internal battery faults.

Contract implications: LTSA §8.2 commits Lighthief to 8-hour on-site attendance for critical alerts. But if the critical fault is internal battery, Lighthief's team can only attend, diagnose externally, and request Linyang remote/on-site support. Resolution clock (Linyang: 5 Business Days = up to 120 hours) significantly exceeds Lighthief's SLA response time. LTSA §8.2(e) clarification on "Response vs Resolution" provides some protection — but client may be frustrated.

Mitigation: (1) Arrange Linyang certification training for Lighthief's field team leads (2 engineers) per Distribution §15. This would allow certified Lighthief engineers to handle routine battery internal maintenance tasks. (2) Add explicit language to LTSA that resolution time for internal battery faults is subject to OEM availability, and that this is documented in the LTSA scope.

T4 — Cycle Life Discrepancy: Cell Spec (6,000) vs. System Contract (7,000)

Rating: LOW — Not a Contract Risk, Explanation Required
Finding: Battery cell spec states 6,000 cycles at 100% DoD, 80% SOH, 0.5C. System warranty and contracts state 7,000 cycles at 90% DoD, 70% EOL (whichever first: 15 years or 7,000 cycles).

Explanation: These are not contradictory — they measure different things. Cell-level spec uses 100% DoD and 80% SOH as end-of-life (stricter). System-level contract uses 90% DoD and 70% EOL (less aggressive cycling, deeper remaining capacity before EOL). At 90% DoD, the cells will reach 7,000+ cycles before reaching 70% SOH, consistent with the contracted warranty. The 15-year term acts as the primary binding constraint (not 7,000 cycles in normal operation at 1 cycle/day = 5,475 cycles over 15 years). RFI V3 confirmed: "15 years OR 7,000 cycles at 70% EOL, 90% DoD, 25±2°C, whichever comes first."

Action: No amendment needed. However, if clients or their engineers raise the cell spec vs. contract cycle life discrepancy, the above explanation should be documented and available. Consider adding a brief technical note to Schedule A of the EPC (or a separate technical annex) clarifying the cycle life measurement basis.

T5 — Monthly Physical Maintenance vs. Bi-Annual LTSA Visits

Rating: MEDIUM — LTSA Service Design Gap
Finding: Linyang's Maintenance Manual requires monthly physical checks (container enclosure, cables, air inlet/outlet). LTSA Tier C provides bi-annual PM visits + 24/7 remote monitoring. Physical monthly items cannot all be done remotely.

Impact: If a warranty claim arises and Linyang requests maintenance records (as required under Warranty Claim Procedure VIII.4), and the records only show bi-annual visits (not monthly), Linyang could challenge the claim as inadequately maintained under Warranty Terms v2 Section III.1.

Mitigation: (a) Remote monitoring covers the BMS data, software logs, and parameter checks monthly (this is the most important item). (b) Add to LTSA client obligations (§14 or Annex): client or designated site operator to perform visual monthly walk-around (check container enclosure, cables, air inlets) and log in the CMMS. Lighthief provides the checklist. This shifts monthly physical inspection to client with Lighthief providing the log template. (c) Add a quarterly light site visit (1 hour per park) for field engineer at additional cost for larger groups.

T6 — Warranty Terms v2 Allows Linyang to Withhold Warranty if Lighthief Has Overdue Payments

Rating: HIGH — Payment Default Creates Downstream Warranty Disruption Risk
Finding: Warranty Terms v2 Section VIII.5: "In the event that the Warranty Right Holder has any overdue payments... Linyang Energy Storage reserves the right to require the Warranty Right Holder to pay a deposit... prior to the implementation of any warranty service resolution."

Impact: If Lighthief delays a DAP payment or SAT payment to Linyang (see cashflow risk in LCY-RISK-001), Linyang can refuse warranty service until a deposit is paid. This means client park outages during a payment dispute would not be remedied by Linyang, putting Lighthief in breach of LTSA availability obligations to the client.

Connection to cashflow risk (S1): A 30-day client pre-ship delay (S1 scenario) that cascades into a Linyang DAP delay would not only create a bridge financing need — it could temporarily suspend Linyang's warranty service obligations to all existing commissioned parks. This is the most severe second-order consequence of a payment delay.

Mitigation: (1) Maintain the working capital facility specifically to ensure Linyang payments are never overdue. (2) In the Sales Contract, add explicit language: "Linyang's warranty service obligations under the separately issued Warranty Terms shall not be suspended or conditioned upon payment of deposits solely due to a payment delay under this Contract that is caused by end-customer payment default, provided Lighthief has notified Linyang of the delay per §6 (End-Customer Payment Provisions)." This aligns with the end-customer payment provisions already in the Sales Contract.
