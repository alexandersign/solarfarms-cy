## 1. Executive Summary

This analysis models the month-by-month cashflow for the Lighthief Cyprus BESS EPC portfolio from contract signing (March 2026) through final acceptance (March 2027). It cross-references client payment terms, Linyang supplier payments, subcontractor obligations, import duties, and VAT at customs clearance.

**Key Finding: The project faces a severe cashflow deficit of approximately €20M between September and November 2026**, driven primarily by:

1. **€15.2M import VAT payable IN CASH at customs** across three batch arrivals (July–September) — Cyprus has NOT implemented postponed accounting, so there is no deferment
2. **~€8.5M in output VAT on client advances/milestones** remitted to the tax department in Q1–Q2 BEFORE any imports occur, creating a double VAT cash drain
3. **Excess input VAT credit (~€8.8M) locked with the government for 8–16 months** under the Cyprus 8-month carry-forward rule before a refund can even be claimed
4. **Structural mismatch** between Linyang's 20% DAP (due at site delivery) and client's 10% PAC (due at commissioning, 2–3 months later)
5. **€2.3M import duty** payable immediately at customs clearance

**The VAT problem is far worse than a simple timing issue.** Total VAT cash locked with the Cyprus government peaks at ~€24M (€8.5M output VAT remitted + €15.2M import VAT paid), with only ~€6.4M offset in Q3. The net excess credit of ~€8.8M requires a VAT 4B refund claim that, under Cyprus law, can only be filed after 8 months of carry-forward — realistically recovering no earlier than H2 2027.

Without structural changes to the contract/import model, Lighthief will require a working capital facility of at least **€20M** to bridge the gap.

## 7. Critical Issues

### ISSUE 1: €15.2M VAT at Customs — No Matching Revenue

**Severity: CRITICAL**

Import VAT of €15.2M is payable at customs clearance across July–September 2026. This is an irrecoverable cost timing issue — the VAT is refundable but Cyprus processes quarterly returns with a 60–120 day lag. The cash is locked for 3–5 months.

At the moment of peak VAT payment (September 2026), there is zero matching client revenue. Batch 3's pre-shipment payment has already been collected in August, and the next client payment (PAC Batch 1) isn't due until October.

### ISSUE 2: Linyang 20% DAP vs Client 10% PAC — Structural Mismatch

**Severity: CRITICAL**

Linyang demands 20% of CIF at site delivery (DAP): **€15,541,000** across July–September 2026.

Clients pay only 10% at PAC (commissioning): **€9,269,000** across October 2026 – February 2027.

This creates two problems:
- **Amount gap**: Lighthief pays €15.5M to Linyang at delivery but only collects €9.3M from clients at PAC — a €6.3M deficit
- **Timing gap**: Linyang DAP is 2–3 months before client PAC (delivery in Jul–Sep vs commissioning in Oct–Feb)

The combined effect: Lighthief must fund ~€15.5M in Linyang DAP payments from its own cash during the same months that VAT consumes another €15.2M.

### ISSUE 3: September 2026 "Cash Cliff"

**Severity: CRITICAL**

September 2026 is the worst month with -€13.1M net outflow and zero inflow:
- Linyang 20% DAP Batch 3: -€5,548K
- Import duty + VAT Batch 3: -€6,260K
- Subcontractors: -€1,243K
- Revenue: €0

This single month accounts for nearly 65% of the peak negative position.

### ISSUE 4: Negative End Position (Before Retention)

**Severity: HIGH**

Even after all PAC payments are collected and VAT is refunded, the project shows a cumulative position of **-€2,541,000** at February 2027. The project only turns positive once the 5% retention (€4,635,000) is released — but that's locked for 24 months post-PAC (earliest release: October 2028 for Batch 1).

This means the gross margin on the active EPC work (~€15M revenue spread) is consumed by:
- VAT timing cost (financing €15.2M for 3–5 months)
- Operational overhead (~€660K/year)
- The structural advance gap (client 30% vs Linyang 25% + Voltus 50% advance)

### ISSUE 5: Single-Quarter VAT Refund Concentration Risk

**Severity: HIGH**

All €15.2M in import VAT falls into a single quarter (Q3 2026). Cyprus tax authorities may:
- Audit the claim given its size relative to the company's history
- Request documentation for the full 246-container import
- Delay processing beyond the normal 60-day window

A 30-day delay in VAT refund extends the peak negative position by ~€15M for an additional month.

### ISSUE 6: ESP 2028 Advance — Ethical & Contractual Risk

**Severity: MEDIUM**

If Esperia signs one contract covering all 11 parks (including 5 for 2028 delivery), the 30% advance on 2028 parks (~€4.97M) would be collected in March 2026 but the corresponding Linyang order won't be placed until 2027/2028. Using this advance to fund 2026 operations creates:
- Risk if the 2028 order is cancelled or modified
- Potential contractual obligation to ring-fence the advance
- Cash accounting issues if advance exceeds 12 months before delivery

## 9. Recommendations

### RECOMMENDATION 1: Adopt 25/50/20/5 Client Payment Terms (HIGH PRIORITY)

Switch from 30/55/10/5 to **25/50/20/5** client terms to align with Linyang upstream terms. This:
- Reduces peak negative by €5.6M
- Eliminates the delivery-stage structural deficit
- Creates a natural pass-through at each milestone
- Shortens the negative cashflow period from 7 to 5 months

The 5% lower advance (€4.6M less upfront) is manageable because March–May have low outflows. Alternatively, negotiate a hybrid: **30/50/15/5** to keep the higher advance while adding a 15% delivery milestone.

### RECOMMENDATION 2: Negotiate Linyang DAP to "After Customs Clearance + 14 Days" (HIGH PRIORITY)

Currently Linyang's 20% is due "when commodities arrive on site." Push for **"14 days after customs clearance confirmation"** rather than immediate DAP. This provides time to:
- Process the customs clearance
- Collect client DAP payments (if Scenario B adopted)
- Arrange VAT financing

Even a 14-day window on €15.5M total DAP payments improves the monthly spread.

### RECOMMENDATION 3: Establish VAT Financing Facility (CRITICAL)

The €15.2M import VAT is the single largest cashflow risk. Options:

| Option | Cost | Benefit |
|--------|------|---------|
| **VAT deferment scheme** | Application to Cyprus Customs | Delay VAT payment by 30–60 days at import. Cyprus offers this for qualifying importers — apply before first shipment. |
| **Import VAT suspension** (temporary admission) | Low/free if qualifying | If equipment is being installed for re-export (not applicable here — for reference only) |
| **Bank VAT facility** | EURIBOR + 2–3% | €15M revolving facility secured against confirmed VAT refund claim. Cost: ~€100K for 4-month bridge |
| **Accelerated VAT refund** | Accountant fees | Request early processing with full documentation pre-filed. Cyprus allows monthly returns for exporters; investigate if EPC qualifies |
| **Stagger imports by 2 weeks** | Logistics cost | Spread B2 and B3 arrivals to push some VAT into Q4, enabling two separate refund claims |

**Recommended approach: Combine VAT deferment application with a €15M bank facility.**

### RECOMMENDATION 4: Restructure Batch Timing to Smooth Cashflow (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

The current schedule creates a "cliff" in September 2026 (zero revenue, three major outflows). Consider:

- **Delay Batch 3 shipment by 3–4 weeks** so it arrives in October instead of September. This moves the VAT/duty into Q4, processed in a separate quarterly return, and coincides with Batch 1 PAC revenue
- **Accelerate Batch 1 by 2 weeks** to create more spacing between batches
- Net effect: Shifts ~€6M in outflows from September to October where Batch 1 PAC (€3.2M) provides partial cover

### RECOMMENDATION 5: Client Pre-Shipment to Include Duty + VAT Component (MEDIUM PRIORITY)

Add a clause in client contracts requiring the 55% (or 50%) pre-shipment payment to include an **additional amount equal to import duty + VAT** for that client's equipment. This is standard in international EPC contracts where the contractor imports on behalf of the client.

For example, ABIO Phase 1 (Batch 1):
- 55% pre-shipment: €17,361K
- Plus duty + VAT allocation: €6,191K
- Total pre-shipment invoice: €23,552K

This directly offsets the customs cashflow hit. The client's total contract price doesn't change — it's simply a timing reallocation.

### RECOMMENDATION 6: Negotiate Linyang Advance Down to 20% (LOW-MEDIUM PRIORITY)

Reduce Linyang advance from 25% to **20%**, offsetting with:
- Increase pre-shipment from 50% to 55%
- This keeps Linyang's pre-delivery total at 75%

Benefit: Frees up €4.3M (5% × €86.0M) at contract signing, providing more buffer for subcontractor advances (Voltus €1.8M) and early operational costs.

### RECOMMENDATION 7: Ring-Fence ESP 2028 Advance (LOW PRIORITY)

If ESP 2028 parks are included in the Esperia contract with advance collected in March 2026:
- Place the ~€5M advance in a separate escrow/deposit account
- Use as collateral for the working capital facility rather than operational cash
- This protects against the risk of having to refund if the 2028 order changes

## 12. DEEP DIVE: Cyprus VAT Cashflow Crisis — Analysis & Solutions

*Added 20 Feb 2026 — Following detailed review of Cyprus VAT Law 95(I)/2000 and EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC*

### 12.1 The Problem: Double VAT Cash Drain

The original cashflow model (Section 6) treated import VAT as a single timing issue — pay at customs, recover via quarterly return. The actual problem is structurally worse because of how Cyprus VAT law handles advance payments and refunds.

**Cyprus VAT does NOT have postponed accounting for imports.** Unlike Ireland, Netherlands, Belgium, and most other EU states that allow import VAT to be declared and recovered on the same VAT return (net zero cash impact), Cyprus requires **cash payment of import VAT at the point of customs clearance** into free circulation. There is no deferment option built into the system.

Simultaneously, under **EU VAT Directive Article 65** (implemented in Cyprus via Law 95(I)/2000 Section 11): **the tax point for advance payments occurs at the moment the payment is received by the supplier.** This means output VAT on client advances is chargeable in Q1 2026 — months before any import VAT credit arises.

### 12.6 Solutions Under Cyprus Law

#### SOLUTION A: Apply for "Repayment Trader" Status — Monthly VAT Returns (IMMEDIATE ACTION)

**Legal basis:** Cyprus allows businesses that regularly have excess input VAT ("repayment traders") to file **monthly returns** instead of quarterly.

**How it helps:**
- Monthly returns mean credits are established faster
- July 2026 return would show Batch 1 import VAT credit immediately
- Offset against August output VAT (Batch 2 pre-ship) within 30 days
- Reduces the carry-forward buildup

**Limitations:**
- The 8-month refund rule still applies to the NET excess credit
- Monthly filing doesn't create an earlier refund right, just faster offset
- Must demonstrate to the Cyprus Tax Department that the company will regularly have excess input VAT
- Lighthief's history (new company, small prior returns) may make approval difficult

**Impact: Reduces peak negative by ~€2–3M through faster monthly offsets, but does NOT solve the core 8-month refund lock.**

**Action required:** Apply to the Cyprus Tax Department (Commissioner of Taxation) before Q2 2026. Provide the import schedule as evidence of expected excess input VAT.

#### SOLUTION B: Customs Bonded Warehouse — Stagger VAT Payments (HIGH IMPACT)

**Legal basis:** EU Customs Code (Regulation 952/2013); Cyprus Customs & Excise Department authorisation for customs warehousing.

**How it works:**
1. Import all containers into a **bonded (customs) warehouse** at Limassol port or nearby
2. While goods are in the bonded warehouse, **NO import VAT or duty is payable** — customs debt is suspended
3. Release containers from the warehouse in controlled batches, triggering VAT/duty only on the released goods
4. Align each release with the quarter where client output VAT invoices will be raised

**Staggered release strategy:**

| Release | Containers | When | Import VAT | Matching Output VAT |
|---------|-----------|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Release 1 | Batch 1 (~80 units) | July 2026 (Q3) | €5,368K | B2+B3 pre-ship output: €6,389K |
| Release 2 | Batch 2 (~70 units) | Aug 2026 (Q3) | €4,410K | (offset within Q3) |
| Release 3 | Batch 3 first half | Oct 2026 (Q4) | €2,714K | PAC B1 output: €600K (partial) |
| Release 4 | Batch 3 second half | Jan 2027 (Q1 27) | €2,714K | PAC B2+B3 output: €1,161K |

**Impact:** By pushing some Batch 3 import VAT into Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, the Q3 2026 excess credit shrinks from €9.2M to ~€4M. Combined with Q4/Q1 output VAT, the carry-forward credit drops to ~€2–3M.

**Costs:**
- Bonded warehouse rental: ~€0.50–€1.50 per m² per month in Limassol

**Net saving: €300K–€370K**

**Risk:** Delays in releasing containers from the bonded warehouse could affect the construction schedule. Must coordinate closely with the construction timeline.

#### SOLUTION C: Client as Importer of Record (ELIMINATES THE PROBLEM)

**Legal basis:** Standard EU customs and VAT law — anyone can be the importer of record provided they are the consignee or have authorisation.

**How it works:**
1. Restructure the EPC contract into two components:

   - **Installation & Commissioning Agreement**: Lighthief provides EPC services (installation, civil works, electrical, commissioning) on the client's equipment.
2. The client pays import VAT and duty at customs clearance
3. The client recovers import VAT on their own VAT return (BESS is a taxable business asset for the client)
4. Lighthief only charges VAT on the service component (much smaller amount)

#### SOLUTION D: Restructure Advance Payment to Defer VAT Tax Point (MEDIUM IMPACT)

**Legal basis:** EU VAT Directive Article 65 — VAT is chargeable on advance payments only when payment is **received by the supplier**. A payment held in escrow or as a true security deposit (refundable, not applied to the contract price) does not trigger a tax point.

**Options:**

**D1: Escrow arrangement for the advance**
- Client pays 30% advance into a joint escrow account (held by a lawyer or bank)
- Funds are released to Lighthief only when equipment is ready to ship (Q2/Q3 2026)
- At release, the tax point arises → output VAT falls in the SAME quarter as import VAT
- Result: Output and input VAT partially offset within Q3, reducing the carry-forward credit

**Problem:** Lighthief needs the advance cash in March to pay Linyang's 25% advance. Escrow defeats this purpose unless Lighthief can use the escrow as collateral for a bank facility to pay Linyang.

**D2: Phased advance payments**
- Instead of 30% at signing (March), collect:
  - 10% at signing (March) → output VAT = €1,760K (much smaller Q1 liability)
  - 20% at production start confirmation (April–May, still Q2) → output VAT = €3,520K in Q2
- This doesn't eliminate the problem but reduces the Q1 output VAT remittance from €5.2M to €1.7M
- Saves €3.5M in early VAT cash outflow

**D3: Structure advance as a "guarantee deposit" rather than payment on account**
- If the advance is characterised as a refundable security deposit (not a prepayment for goods/services), under CJEU case law it does not trigger Article 65
- The deposit is only reclassified as a payment when it is applied against a milestone invoice
- **Risk:** Cyprus Tax Dept may challenge this characterisation, especially if the deposit is non-refundable in practice or if it is applied automatically against the first invoice

**Impact: D1 or D3 could shift ~€5M in output VAT from Q1 to Q3, reducing the net credit to ~€4M and cutting the locked-up cash by almost half.**

#### SOLUTION E: Capital Goods Argument for Immediate Refund (UNCERTAIN)

**Legal basis:** Cyprus VAT Law Section 20 allows immediate refund (not subject to the 8-month rule) for **input VAT relating to capital asset purchases of the company**.

**Argument:** The BESS containers, PCS units, and transformers are capital equipment with a useful life of 15+ years. Even though Lighthief is installing them for clients, during the construction period Lighthief holds title to the goods and they could be classified as capital assets in Lighthief's balance sheet.

**Counter-argument:** Lighthief is an EPC contractor purchasing goods for resale/installation — these are trading stock, not capital assets. The goods are never used in Lighthief's own business; they are delivered to clients.

**Assessment: Weak argument. Not recommended as a primary strategy, but worth discussing with the tax advisor as a supplementary claim.**

---

#### SOLUTION F: VAT Group Registration with Clients (COMPLEX)

**Legal basis:** EU VAT Directive Article 11 allows Member States to treat closely bound persons as a single taxable person (VAT group). Cyprus has implemented VAT grouping.

**How it works:** If Lighthief and a client entity form a VAT group, supplies between them are disregarded for VAT purposes. The import would be treated as a supply to the group, and the subsequent installation/delivery to the client is an internal transaction — no VAT.

**Assessment: Highly unlikely to be practical.** VAT grouping requires close financial, economic, and organisational links. Lighthief and its clients are independent parties. This would only work if Lighthief had an equity stake in the client entities.
