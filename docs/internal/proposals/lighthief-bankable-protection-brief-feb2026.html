Liquidated Damages — Delay Protection

Delay LDs distinguish between full park delays (Critical Path Equipment) and partial delays (individual battery containers), ensuring the LD exposure is proportionate to the actual impact on commissioning.

Delay TypeTriggerLD BaseRateCap
Full Park DelayPCS, MV transformer, or MV switchgear lateEntire park contract value0.1% → 0.15% → 0.2%/day (tiered)10% of park value
Partial DelayBattery container(s) lateValue of delayed goods only0.1% → 0.15% → 0.2%/day (tiered)10% of delayed goods value
Worked Example — Full Park Delay: A €2M park has a PCS delayed by 45 days beyond agreed delivery.
Days 1–30: 30 × 0.1% × €2,000,000 = €60,000
Days 31–45: 15 × 0.15% × €2,000,000 = €45,000
Total LD: €105,000 (5.25% of contract value)

Worked Example — Partial Delay: 1 of 4 battery containers (value €250,000) delayed by 20 days.
20 × 0.1% × €250,000 = €5,000 (LD on delayed goods only; park partially operational)

Worked Example 1 — Minor Shortfall (20 MWh system, Year 5):
Guaranteed SOH: 85% · Actual SOH: 83% (2% shortfall)
Lost Capacity = 2% × 20 MWh = 0.4 MWh
Daily LD = 0.4 × €30 = €12/day
Grace period: 90 days (no LDs accrue). If unremedied for 120 days past grace: €12 × 120 = €1,440

Worked Example 2 — Significant Shortfall (100 MWh system, Year 10):
Guaranteed SOH: 79.58% · Actual SOH: 75% (4.58% shortfall)
Lost Capacity = 4.58% × 100 MWh = 4.58 MWh
Daily LD = 4.58 × €30 = €137.40/day
If unremedied for 180 days past grace: €137.40 × 180 = €24,732 (subject to annual cap)

Bankability rationale: The €30/MWh/day rate is the same metric used for availability LDs, enabling lenders to model a single LD rate across both availability and SOH performance. The formula is deterministic, measurable, time-pressured (90-day remedy), and capped — meeting standard project finance requirements.

12.9 BESS Utilization & Degradation Impact

How degradation affects the data-validated model:

Unlike a flat-assumption model, this projection uses actual daily curtailment data to determine BESS fill each day.
At Year 1 (SOH 94.6%), the 17.03 MWh usable capacity captures 3,755 MWh/year across 365 days. After 86.32% RTE: 3,241 MWh discharged.
At Year 15 (SOH 73.6%), usable capacity drops to 13.25 MWh — but this still exceeds the daily curtailment on 303 of 365 days. After RTE: 2,877 MWh discharged.

The degradation impact is moderate: Year 15 captures 3,333 MWh vs Year 1's 3,755 MWh — an 11.2% decline over 15 years.
This decline comes entirely from the 62 highest-curtailment days (mostly Mar–May) where the degraded BESS can no longer fully capture the excess.

Revenue remains curtailment-driven, not degradation-driven. The BESS has surplus capacity on 83% of days even at end-of-life. All values use 86.32% full-system AC-AC RTE (incl. cabling losses).

Cycle Life Analysis
