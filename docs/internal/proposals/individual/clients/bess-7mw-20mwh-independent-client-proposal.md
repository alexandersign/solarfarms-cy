## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal presents a detailed financial analysis for deploying a **7 MW / 20 MWh Battery Energy Storage System (BESS)** alongside a **7 MWp fixed-tilt bifacial PV park** in Cyprus. All market data is sourced exclusively from **134 verified TSOC Day-Ahead Market (DAM) Excel files** covering every trading day from **1 October 2025 to 11 February 2026** — the complete dataset since the Cyprus Competitive Electricity Market launch.

The analysis demonstrates that BESS deployed for **curtailment recovery** — the only legal revenue model for BESS in Cyprus as of February 2026 — delivers a compelling return on investment, transforming otherwise-wasted solar energy into peak-priced revenue.

Additionally, Lighthief Cyprus offers to **acquire Ready-to-Build (RTB) stage solar projects at a maximum price of €350,000 per MW**.

## 3. Regulatory Context

> **As of February 2026, BESS in Cyprus cannot buy electricity from the DAM grid.**

BESS revenue is legally limited to **curtailment recovery only**:

- **Charge cost = €0** — energy is stored from the park's own curtailed solar production (DSO dispatch-down orders)
- **Discharge at peak evening prices** — verified average **€182.99/MWh**
- Grid arbitrage (buy at midday, sell at peak) requires new legislation — expected but **not yet enacted**
- Future arbitrage, when legal, will provide **additional** revenue on top of curtailment recovery

## 6. Financial Model — BESS Curtailment Recovery

### 6.1 BESS Revenue Calculation

Under the current Cyprus regulatory framework, BESS charges exclusively from **curtailed solar** (charge cost = €0) and discharges at **verified peak evening prices** (€182.99/MWh average, 134 days of data).

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Charge cost** | €0/MWh | Curtailed energy — would be wasted |
| **Discharge price** | €182.99/MWh | Verified peak avg (17:00–21:00), 134 days |
| **RTE** | 86.32% | Full system AC-AC incl. cabling |
| **Revenue per MWh stored** | **€157.96/MWh** | For each MWh of curtailed solar charged, €157.96 is earned at discharge (€182.99 × 0.8632) |

#### How much curtailed energy can BESS capture?

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Annual curtailed energy from 7 MW park | 2,980 MWh |
| BESS capacity per cycle | 20 MWh |
| Max annual BESS absorption (1 cycle/day) | 7,300 MWh |
| **Actual BESS utilisation** | **2,980 MWh charged** (limited by curtailment, not capacity) |
| Energy discharged after RTE (86.32%) | **2,572 MWh** |
| **Annual discharge revenue** | **2,572 MWh × €182.99 = €470,696** |

The 20 MWh BESS is **more than sufficient** to absorb all curtailed energy from a 7 MW park. The battery does not need to cycle fully every day — it only needs to capture what the DSO curtails.

#### Daily Profile (Average)

| Period | Activity | Volume |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **10:00–14:00** | Charge from curtailed solar | ~8.2 MWh/day avg (varies by season) |
| **17:00–21:00** | Discharge at peak prices | ~7.0 MWh/day avg (after RTE) |

- Curtailment is **100% correlated with solar production** — it cannot be avoided
- **100% of days** (134/134) showed positive peak-midday spread — BESS is profitable every single day

## 9. Future Revenue Upside — DAM Arbitrage (Not Yet Legal)

When legislation enables BESS to buy electricity from the DAM, an additional revenue stream opens:

| Parameter | Verified Value |
|-----------|---------------|
| Buy price (midday, 10:00–14:00) | €101.13/MWh |
| Sell price (peak, 17:00–21:00) | €182.99/MWh |
| Gross spread | €81.86/MWh |
| Net per MWh after RTE (86.32%) | **€70.66/MWh** |

This would allow the BESS to generate revenue even on days when curtailment is insufficient to fill the battery (e.g., winter months with only €50/MWh spread). With a 20 MWh battery cycling fully via arbitrage on non-curtailment days:

| Scenario | Additional Annual Revenue |
|----------|-------------------------:|
| 100 arbitrage days/year × 20 MWh | **€121,990** |
