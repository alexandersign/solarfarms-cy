Executive Summary

Operating model for Lighthief Cyprus BESS O&M operations

This Workflow Plan defines the operational procedures for Lighthief Cyprus's BESS O&M team, covering a portfolio of 51 parks totalling approximately 882 MWh. The team operates from Limassol, with all park locations reachable within 1 hour maximum drive time.

The plan is built around a strict 97% availability guarantee with a critical constraint: only 2 days (48 hours) of operational downtime are permitted per park per year. All planned maintenance (8 days/park/year) is performed during the midnight–6am window, completely outside operational hours.

The model deploys 5 field engineers on a rolling night/day rotation, ensuring continuous coverage for both planned maintenance and reactive fault response.

CRITICAL FINANCIAL RISK

A 20 MWh park that breaches the 2-day downtime limit faces €1,000/day in client LDs (€50 × 20 MWh) plus €600/day in Linyang LDs (€30 × 20 MWh). One bad week of simultaneous faults without enough engineers costs more than a full year’s salary. Speed of response is the #1 priority.

Rotation ensures no engineer works nights and days on the same day. Each engineer averages 2–3 night shifts per week, with full rest days built in. Daily Workflow

Standard operating timeline for both night and day operations

Night Crew — Planned Maintenance

22:00 — Pre-Shift
Preparation & Route Planning
Review assigned parks for the night. Check monitoring data for anomalies. Load tools, consumables, and test equipment. Confirm route and park access.
23:00 — Depart
Travel to First Park
Drive from Limassol base. All parks reachable within 1 hour. Batch nearby parks to minimise travel.
00:00 — Maintenance Window Opens
Begin Planned Maintenance
Execute OEM bi-annual maintenance checklist. Container inspection, electrical checks, battery readings, thermal imaging, BMS data download, safety systems test. Follow Linyang manual precisely.
03:00 — Complete / Move
Complete Park 1, Move to Park 2
If batching small nearby parks, travel to second park. Typical maintenance per park: 2.5–3.5 hours.
06:00 — Window Closes
End of Maintenance Window
All systems must be returned to full operational state by 06:00. Submit maintenance report. Log all readings, findings, and any follow-up items.

Day Crew — Reactive Response

06:00 — Shift Start
Handover from Night Crew
Review night shift reports. Check overnight alarm log. Confirm all parks operational. Note any flagged follow-up items from night maintenance.
07:00 — Morning Review
Daily Monitoring & Triage
Review 24-hour monitoring data across all 51 parks. Triage any alerts with Poland NOC. Prioritise reactive calls based on severity and SLA impact.
On Alarm — Immediate
Reactive Fault Response
Acknowledge alarm within 30 minutes. Attempt remote fix with NOC. If on-site required: dispatch engineer (max 1 hr travel). Target: resolve within 4–6 hours to minimise downtime impact.
16:00 — Reporting
Daily Report & Documentation
Complete service reports for any reactive calls. Update warranty claim tracker. Log parts used. Update availability tracker per park.
22:00 — Shift End
Handover to Night Crew
Brief night crew on any outstanding issues. Log any parks requiring attention during night window. Transition to on-call (emergency only) if needed.

SCHEDULING RULE

Each park receives 8 planned maintenance nights per year. At 1–2 parks per night with 2 engineers, total annual night shifts = ~320. Across 5 engineers rotating, each engineer works ~130 night shifts/year (~2.5/week).

RESPONSE TIME TARGET

From alarm to engineer on-site: <90 minutes. From alarm to resolution: <6 hours for standard faults. Every hour of downtime during operational hours = 1/48th of the annual budget consumed. There is no room for delayed response.

AVAILABILITY TRACKING RULE

Every park's availability is tracked individually. If any single park exceeds 24 hours of downtime in H1, it is flagged as at risk and receives priority scheduling for the remainder of the year. The O&M Manager must prepare a remediation plan within 48 hours of the flag.

LETHAL VOLTAGE WARNING

Lethal voltages (up to 1,497.6V DC) are present inside BESS containers at all times. Wait 5 minutes after power disconnect before any work. Always use a standard voltmeter to confirm zero voltage. Follow Linyang User Manual safety procedures without exception.
