Batch 1: VAT & Cashflow Context

Cyprus has NOT implemented postponed accounting for import VAT. Under the 8-month carry-forward rule (VAT Law Section 20), excess input VAT credit cannot be refunded until 8 months after the quarter in which it arises. For Batch 1: first payment 1 April 2026 keeps Q1 clean; civil works (concrete platforms and trenches) must be paid by June 2026.

April 1 Start: First Invoice & Payment on or after 1 April

Under EU VAT Directive Article 65, the tax point is the earliest of supply, invoice, or payment. Both invoice and payment must fall on or after 1 April for Q1 to be clean (€0). Linyang 30% advance (accepted 30/50/10/10) due on or after 1 April to align with client 30% receipt.

All costs ex-VAT; subcontractor VAT offsets advance

All figures in this document (Linyang, civil works, adders, ops) are ex-VAT. Subcontractors (including civil works — platforms & trenches) charge VAT on their invoices. We recover this as input VAT and offset it against output VAT (e.g. on the client 30% advance) in the same quarter. For Batch 1, civil works paid by June fall in Q2 2026, so the input VAT on civil works (€11K) reduces Q2 net VAT payable.

VAT Solutions Research — Cyprus

Postponed accounting (Article 211): Cyprus has not implemented it — import VAT is paid at clearance. Advance payments: Place of supply remains Cyprus when the importer is in Cyprus, so VAT is due on advances. Polish company structure: Using a Polish entity as importer (Poland has PVA) then intra-community supply to Cyprus (reverse charge in Cyprus) does avoid cash at both borders. See vat-solutions-cyprus-research.md for sources and details.
