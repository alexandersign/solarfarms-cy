## 1. Market Overview

Cyprus launched its **Competitive Electricity Market (CEM)** on **1 October 2025**, marking a historic transition from a single-buyer monopoly (operated by the Electricity Authority of Cyprus / EAC) to a liberalized, rules-based market operated by the **Transmission System Operator of Cyprus (TSOC / ΔΣΜΚ)**.

### Key Facts

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Market Launch** | 1 October 2025 |
| **Market Operator** | TSOC (ΔΣΜΚ) |
| **Regulator** | CERA (ΡΑΕΚ) |
| **Model** | EU Target Model |
| **Trading Intervals** | 30-minute periods |
| **Market Type** | Non-interconnected (isolated island system) |
| **Initial Participants** | 17 entities |
| **Wholesale Price Cap** | €500/MWh |
| **New Market Avg MCP (Oct-Feb)** | €165.12/MWh (from 21 sample days) |
| **ENTSO-E Membership** | Full member since October 2005 |

### Pre-Market History

- **1996:** EU Directive 96/92/EC on common rules for the internal electricity market
- **2003:** Law N.122(I)/2003 - Regulation of the Electricity Market Act
- **2004:** TSOC established and began operations (17 September 2004)
- **2012:** Law N.211(I)/2012 - TSOC renamed to TSOC-Cyprus, designated as legal entity of public law
- **2015:** CERA Regulatory Decision RAA 164/2015 - Detailed Planning for Market Diversification
- **2021:** Law N.130(I)/2021 - New Electricity Market Regulation Act (supersedes all 2003-2018 laws)
- **2022:** TSOC granted Market Operator License (30-year term: 7 Oct 2022 - 6 Oct 2052)
- **2023-2025:** Dry run testing phases (first, second, third dry runs)
- **1 October 2025:** Market goes live

### Market Rules (Κανόνες Αγοράς Ηλεκτρισμού)

The Electricity Market Rules:
1. **Govern** the mechanisms, pricing, and other terms for electricity purchase/sale
2. **Ensure non-discrimination** among license holders
3. **Promote** efficiency, economy, and competition

All license holders and exempted persons must comply. Rules are published in the CERA Legislation section under "Rules / Electricity."

### Transmission and Distribution Rules

The Transmission and Distribution Rules:
1. **Address** technical requirements for connection to the transmission/distribution system
2. **Ensure** non-discriminatory technical conditions
3. **Promote** efficiency, reliability, and economy in system use and development

## 3. Regulatory Bodies

### CERA - Cyprus Energy Regulatory Authority (ΡΑΕΚ)

**Role:** Independent regulatory authority overseeing all aspects of the electricity (and natural gas) market.

**Key responsibilities:**
- Approve and revise tariffs and charging policies
- Issue, amend, extend, and revoke licenses
- Approve Transmission and Distribution Rules
- Approve Electricity Market Rules
- Regulate market integrity (REMIT implementation)
- Protect consumer interests
- Oversee Guarantees of Origin system
- Set performance indicators for suppliers and DSO

**Key portal:** [portal.cera.org.cy](https://portal.cera.org.cy) - Electronic Application Submission Portal (launched 3 December 2025)

### TSOC - Transmission System Operator of Cyprus (ΔΣΜΚ)

**Role:** Operates the transmission system and serves as Market Operator.

**Dual mandate:**
1. **System Operator:** Development, management, and operation of the transmission system
2. **Market Operator:** Management and operation of the wholesale electricity market

*(See Section 4 for detailed organizational structure)*

### EAC - Electricity Authority of Cyprus (ΑΗΚ)

**Roles in the new market:**
- **Transmission System Owner** (ΙΣΜ)
- **Distribution System Owner** (ΙΣΔ)
- **Distribution System Operator** (ΔΣΔ) - designated by EAC
- **Licensed Supplier** (currently the only licensed retail supplier)
- **Ultimate Supplier** (supplier of last resort, per Article 106)
- **Dominant Producer** (~78% of production, Feb 2025)

## 5. Market Structure & Trading Timeframes

The Cyprus market follows the **EU Target Model** with four trading timeframes:

### 5.1 Forward Market (FM)
- **Type:** Long-term bilateral over-the-counter (OTC) contracts
- **Timing:** Weeks or months ahead of delivery
- **Purpose:** Price stability, hedging exposure to short-term fluctuations
- **October 2025:** 34% of total energy was supplied through Forward Market contracts
- **Must-run units:** Covered ~38% of total energy volumes at ~€190/MWh average cost

### 5.2 Day-Ahead Market (DAM)
- **Type:** Central auction, competitive bidding
- **Timing:** Participants submit offers/bids for each **30-minute interval** of the following day
- **Mechanism:** Market Operator (TSOC) matches supply and demand → determines **Market Clearing Price (MCP)** and generation schedule
- **October 2025:** 51% of total energy from conventional DAM + 7% from RES DAM
- **Data Reports:** Published daily at [tsoc.org.cy - DAM Reports](https://tsoc.org.cy/competitive-electricity-market/third-dryrun/reports/day-ahead-market-daily-activity-reports-el/)

### 5.3 Intra-Day Market (IDM)
- **Type:** Continuous trading after DAM results
- **Timing:** Between DAM closure and real-time delivery
- **Purpose:** Adjust positions based on updated forecasts or availability changes
- **Status:** **Not yet active** in Cyprus (expected in next phase of market development)

### 5.4 Balancing Market (BM)
- **Type:** Real-time balancing managed by TSOC
- **Purpose:** Correct remaining imbalances between supply and demand in real time
- **Participants:** Balancing Service Providers (BSPs) provide balancing and ancillary services
- **Responsibility:** Balancing Responsible Parties (BRPs) are responsible for deviations between scheduled and actual generation/consumption

### How Demand is Met (Progressive Coverage)

```
Forward Market → Day-Ahead Market → Intra-Day Market → Balancing Market
(Long-term       (Next-day           (Near real-time    (Real-time
 contracts)       auction)            adjustments)       corrections)
```

## 8. Trading & Settlement Rules

### Day-Ahead Market Bidding

- Participants submit **offers** (generators) and **bids** (suppliers/consumers) for each 30-minute interval of the following day
- TSOC's Market Management System determines the **Market Clearing Price (MCP)** through marginal pricing
- All accepted offers receive the MCP (pay-as-cleared)
- Price cap: **€500/MWh**
- Price floor: **€0/MWh**

### Must-Run Requirements

- Certain conventional units must remain online for **inertia, frequency control, and reserve capacity**
- Cyprus has no storage, interconnections, or other technologies to provide these services
- Must-run units covered **~38%** of total energy volumes in October 2025
- Average must-run cost: **~€190/MWh**
- This cost is **socialized** across suppliers (purchased through Forward Market proportionally)
- Creates a **systemic cost floor**, limiting ability to transfer wholesale savings to consumers

### Balancing and Settlement

- **BRPs** are financially responsible for imbalances between scheduled and actual positions
- **BSPs** provide balancing energy and ancillary services
- Balancing follows EU Regulation 2017/2195 (harmonized technical standards)
- Settlement occurs after delivery based on metered data

### Network Tariffs

CERA-regulated tariffs include:
- Transmission System usage tariff
- Medium Voltage Distribution System usage tariff
- Low Voltage Distribution System usage tariff
- TSOC cost recovery tariff
- Ancillary Services and Long-Term Reserve tariff

All tariffs are calculated per CERA Regulatory Decision 02/2015 (KDP 208/2015).

### Retail Tariffs (EAC, effective August 2025)

**Single Rate (Code 01):**
- Energy: 10.34 c/kWh + Network: 3.66 c/kWh + Ancillary: 0.65 c/kWh = **~14.65 c/kWh**

**Two-Rate (Code 02) - Peak/Off-Peak:**
- Peak (09:00-23:00): 10.76 + 3.66 + 0.65 = **~15.07 c/kWh**
- Off-Peak (23:00-09:00): 9.44 + 3.66 + 0.65 = **~13.75 c/kWh**

*Based on fuel cost of €300/MT with fuel adjustment clause.*

### Key Findings vs Pre-Market Predictions

> **CRITICAL UPDATE**: The actual market data shows **significantly less price volatility** than pre-market estimates suggested. Pre-launch models predicted solar midday prices of €0-45/MWh and evening peaks of €200-260/MWh, creating a €150-260/MWh spread. In reality, the spread is only **€32/MWh on average**.

| What Was Predicted | What Actually Happened |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| Solar hours: €0-45/MWh | Solar hours: **€150/MWh** |
| Peak hours: €200-260/MWh | Peak hours: **€182/MWh** |
| Spread: €150-260/MWh | Spread: **€32/MWh** |
| Zero-price frequency: 42% of days | Zero-price: **~2.2% of periods** |
| Price cap events: €500/MWh | Max observed: **€200/MWh** |

### Price Patterns - Reality vs Predictions

The Cyprus open market operates with **much more uniform pricing** than expected:

**Solar hours are NOT near-zero:**
- Solar hours average €150/MWh, not €0-45/MWh
- Zero-price periods are rare (~2.2%), not common (42%)
- Solar penetration: 5-13% depending on season (still modest)

**Evening peak premiums are modest:**
- Evening peak (17:00-21:00): €175-195/MWh, not €200-280/MWh
- The premium over daytime is only ~20% on average
- Driven by limited generation diversity (conventional still dominates)

**Seasonal trends are emerging:**
- Winter (Jan-Feb): Higher baseline prices (€174/MWh avg), lower solar penetration (5-7%)
- Autumn (Oct): More volatile, higher spread (€46/MWh), more solar (11%)
- Spread is NARROWING over time as market matures (€46 → €19 Oct-Jan)

### BESS Revenue Model — Current vs Future

> **⚠️ REGULATORY NOTE (February 2026):** BESS cannot buy electricity from the Cyprus DAM grid. Grid arbitrage (buying at solar-hour prices and selling at peak) is **NOT yet legal**. The enabling legislation has not been passed as of February 2026.

#### Current Revenue: Curtailment Recovery (Active Now)

BESS revenue in Cyprus today comes exclusively from **curtailment recovery** — storing solar energy that the DSO orders to be curtailed (charge cost = €0, energy would otherwise be wasted) and discharging at evening peak prices.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Charge Cost** | **€0/MWh** (curtailed solar — would be wasted) |
| **Discharge Price (peak hours avg)** | **€182/MWh** (verified TSOC DAM data) |
| **Net Revenue per MWh discharged** | **€159.8/MWh** (after 87.8% RTE) |
| **Curtailment hours** | ~10:00-14:00 (peak solar, DSO dispatch-down orders) |
| **Discharge hours** | ~17:00-21:00 (evening peak demand) |

**Annual Revenue by Curtailment Level (per MWh of BESS capacity):**

| Curtailment Rate | Annual Revenue/MWh BESS | Source |
|:----------------:|:-----------------------:|--------|
| 15% | ~€9,900 | Low-curtailment parks |
| 25% | ~€16,500 | Cyprus average (25.8%) |
| 38% | ~€28,000 | Anarita 10MW real data |
| 45% | ~€29,700 | High-curtailment parks |

*Assumptions: 5MWp solar per 1.25MW/5MWh BESS (4:1 ratio), 1650 kWh/kWp yield, 87.8% RTE, €182/MWh peak price*

| **Net per Cycle (after RTE)** | ~€10/MWh |
| **Additional Annual Revenue/MWh** | ~€3,600-4,000 |

> **Key insight**: Curtailment recovery is **far more profitable** than grid arbitrage because the charge cost is €0 (vs €150/MWh from the DAM). This makes the current regulatory constraint less impactful than it might appear — BESS economics in Cyprus are primarily driven by curtailment, not price arbitrage.

## 10. Transmission & Distribution System

### Transmission System
- **Definition:** High voltage power lines for electricity transmission between plants and substations
- **Owner:** EAC (as Transmission System Owner)
- **Operator:** TSOC (as Transmission System Operator)
- **Regulator:** CERA (approves tariffs, rules, charging policy)

### Distribution System
- **Definition:** Medium and low voltage power lines for distribution to end customers
- **Owner:** EAC (as Distribution System Owner)
- **Operator:** EAC (as Distribution System Operator, designated per law)
- **Regulator:** CERA

### System Characteristics
- **Isolated island system** - no interconnections to neighboring grids
- No ability to import or export electricity
- Supply and demand must always be balanced locally
- Higher price volatility than interconnected systems
- Greater dependence on domestic flexibility resources (storage, demand response)
- Critical dependence on accurate forecasting and efficient scheduling

## 11. Renewable Energy Sources (RES)

### RES Types in Cyprus
- **Photovoltaic Systems** (dominant)
- **Wind Systems**
- **Biomass**
- **Solar Thermal Systems**

### Installed Capacity
- Total RES installed capacity exceeds **1,000 MW** (as of Oct 2025)
- Despite this, only 14% of traded electricity came from renewables in the first month

### National Targets
- **Power generation:** 16% from RES
- **Heating/cooling:** 23.5% from RES
- **Transport:** 10% from RES

### Guarantees of Origin
- Electronic registry managed by TSOC (authorized issuer per CERA Decision 857/2013)
- Issued for: RES production and high-efficiency CHP
- Process: Issue → Transfer → Cancel
- Supervised by CERA for objectivity and transparency
- Registry: [gocy.dsm.org.cy](https://gocy.dsm.org.cy/Default.aspx?language=1)

### RES Procurement Pricing
- Monthly EAC fuel cost-based calculation
- Basic purchase price = EAC fuel cost + variable maintenance cost
- Fuel clause adjusted for system losses
- Methodology approved by CERA

### Curtailment Challenge
- Curtailment surged from **0% to 45.8%** in four years (2021-2025)
- October 2025: ~2.2% of half-hourly periods hit zero price (much less than pre-market predictions of 42%)
- No grid-scale storage → excess solar is curtailed
- Key driver for BESS investment

## 12. Energy Storage (BESS) in the Market

### Licensing for Storage
- Per **Article 26(m)** of Law N.130(I)/2021: Installing and/or operating an electricity storage facility requires a CERA license
- **Exception:** Storage facilities operated exclusively for own use
- Storage operators can participate in the market as producers, aggregators, or BSPs

### Current Status (February 2026)
- **No utility-scale storage** operational yet — Lighthief BESS projects are among the first in planning
- **BESS cannot buy from the DAM** — grid arbitrage legislation has NOT been passed as of Feb 2026
- BESS revenue today comes exclusively from **curtailment recovery** (storing own curtailed solar)
- Expected to be a key factor in reducing price volatility and wholesale costs once deployed

### Revenue Model — Current vs Future

**Current (Feb 2026) — Curtailment Recovery:**
- BESS stores otherwise-curtailed solar energy (charge cost = **€0**)
- Discharges at evening peak prices (**€182/MWh** verified TSOC data)
- Revenue: **€16,500–28,000/MWh BESS/year** depending on curtailment level (25–38%)
- This is highly profitable because charge cost is zero

**Future — When Legislation Enables BESS Market Participation:**
- **DAM Arbitrage:** Buy at solar-hour prices (~€150/MWh), sell at peak (~€182/MWh)
  - Additional spread: ~€32/MWh → ~€3,600–4,000/MWh BESS/year
- **Balancing Market:** Provide frequency response and reserve services
- **Capacity Payments:** Revenue for availability guarantees
- Expected wholesale price reduction of **10-15%** once storage is deployed (per Poullikkas 2026)

## 13. Market Challenges & Outlook

### Current Challenges (per Poullikkas 2026 analysis)

1. **Extreme price volatility** - characteristic of isolated systems (€0 to €500/MWh)
2. **High market concentration** - only 17 participants, EAC dominates
3. **Shallow liquidity** - insufficient competition
4. **Heavy conventional reliance** - 86% fossil despite 1,000+ MW RES installed
5. **Must-run cost burden** - embedded cost floor of ~€190/MWh socialized across market
6. **No natural gas** - infrastructure still under construction
7. **No utility-scale storage** - unable to absorb solar surplus
8. **Electrical isolation** - no interconnections for import/export flexibility
9. **Incomplete smart metering** - limits demand-side participation

### Projected Improvements

| Scenario | Wholesale Price Reduction | Timeline |
|----------|:------------------------:|----------|
| **Base Case** | -12.5% | By end of 2027 |
| **Optimistic Case** | -15% | By end of 2027 |

**Dependencies for improvement:**
- Natural gas commissioning (replace expensive oil)
- Grid-scale battery storage deployment
- Regulatory reform (must-run optimization)
- New market entrants (increase competition)
- Smart metering rollout (enable demand response)

### International Benchmarking
- Similar newly liberalized markets (Greece, Ireland, Portugal) typically require **18-30 months** to develop liquidity, stability, and genuine competition
- Cyprus is on a similar trajectory
