## 2. Import VAT Concern

Under current Cyprus VAT legislation, import VAT at 19% is payable in full at the point of customs clearance. For the first shipment alone, this represents approximately **EUR 2,577,000** in import VAT, payable in cash before the goods can be released from the port.

While we understand that this import VAT is fully recoverable as input VAT, the timing mismatch between payment at customs and recovery through the VAT return cycle creates a significant working capital burden. We estimate that the full portfolio will generate approximately **EUR 16,800,000** in import VAT over the project lifecycle.

## 3. Specific Questions

We respectfully request your guidance on the following:

### 3.1 Postponed Accounting (EU VAT Directive Article 211(b))

We are aware that several EU Member States (including the Netherlands, Belgium, and Ireland) have implemented postponed accounting for import VAT, whereby the import VAT is declared and simultaneously recovered on the same VAT return, resulting in a net zero cash impact.

**Question:** Has Cyprus implemented, or does it plan to implement, any form of postponed accounting for import VAT under Article 211(b) of EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC? If not, is there any alternative mechanism that achieves a similar cash-neutral outcome for VAT-registered importers?

### 3.2 Import VAT Deferment

Some EU Member States offer deferment schemes whereby import VAT is not payable at the point of customs clearance but is instead declared on the periodic VAT return.

**Question:** Does Cyprus operate any import VAT deferment scheme, whether general or sector-specific (e.g., for renewable energy or capital equipment)? If so, what are the eligibility criteria and application procedure?

### 3.3 Monthly VAT Return Filing (Repayment Trader Status)

We understand that under Section 11 of VAT Law 95(I)/2000, the Commissioner may authorise a taxable person to file VAT returns on a monthly basis where the business regularly generates excess input VAT.

**Question:** We wish to apply for monthly VAT return filing effective from 1 July 2026. Could you please confirm the application procedure, required documentation, and expected processing time? Is there a specific form or application template?

### 3.4 Carry-Forward and Refund of Excess Input VAT

Under Section 20 of the VAT Law, excess input VAT is carried forward and may be refunded after a specified period.

**Question:** Could you please confirm the current rules governing the refund of excess input VAT credits? Specifically:
- After how many consecutive months/quarters of credit does a taxpayer become eligible for a direct refund?
- Is there an expedited refund process for large-value imports related to capital investment projects?
- Is there any provision for pre-approval or accelerated processing of refund claims exceeding EUR 500,000?

### 3.5 Sector-Specific Relief for Renewable Energy

Given the Republic of Cyprus's obligations under the EU Clean Energy Package, the National Energy and Climate Plan (NECP), and the urgent need for energy storage infrastructure to support the Cyprus electricity grid, we wish to inquire whether any VAT incentives or relief measures exist — or are being considered — specifically for renewable energy and energy storage projects.

**Question:** Are there any existing or planned VAT exemptions, reduced rates, or administrative facilitations for the import of energy storage equipment intended for grid-connected renewable energy installations in Cyprus?

## 4. About the Project

This project involves the deployment of 887 MWh of battery energy storage capacity across 51 licensed solar parks in all five districts of Cyprus. The project directly supports:

The total project investment (equipment, civil works, installation, and commissioning) exceeds EUR 100,000,000, representing one of the largest private-sector energy infrastructure investments in Cyprus.
