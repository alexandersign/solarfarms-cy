1. Current Enforcement Environment

The Electricity Authority of Cyprus (EAC) publicly acknowledged in March 2024 that unauthorized PV installations are a widespread issue across both commercial and residential sectors in Cyprus. EAC spokeswoman Christina Papadopoulou confirmed that enforcement is currently reactive and based on tip-offs:

"There are a lot of rooftops and it is difficult to inspect each one. Now we act mainly on tip-offs." — Christina Papadopoulou, EAC Spokeswoman, Cyprus Mail, 26 March 2024

There is no active EAC inspection program targeting unauthorized PV installations. The authority does not conduct routine audits or site visits to identify non-compliant systems.

2. Industry Precedent

Leading commercial PV installers operating in Cyprus — including firms with decades of market presence — report that they have multiple installations operating in a self-consumption / zero-export configuration without formal EAC applications.

The consistent industry experience is that when such installations are discovered, the typical outcome is:

  • A warning letter from EAC
  • A requirement to regularize the installation (submit a proper application)
  • No criminal prosecution or significant financial penalties

There are no published court cases, prosecution records, or specific fine amounts for unauthorized PV installations in Cyprus.

3. EAC's Stated Enforcement Measures

EAC has publicly stated that its enforcement measures for unauthorized installations consist of:

Measure Description
System disconnection The illegal system is disconnected from the grid
Certificate cancellation The approval certificate is revoked
Fines Fines may be imposed (no specific amounts have been published)

Under the Electricity Regulations (Cap. 82), connecting an installation without prior inspection is classified as an offence, but no penalty schedule has been published for this category of violation.

Once a smart meter is installed at your premises, EAC would theoretically be able to observe changes in your daytime grid consumption pattern. A significant reduction in daytime grid import could indicate the presence of on-site generation.

5. Generator Factor — Your Specific Situation

How the Existing Diesel Generator Affects Detection Risk

Your premises at Greenland Family Park already operate a diesel generator connected to the facility's electrical system. This is a significant factor in the risk profile:

  1. Existing irregular load profile. The generator already creates periods of reduced or zero grid import when running. EAC's meter — whether smart or conventional — already records an irregular consumption pattern for your site. This is normal baseline behavior.
  2. PV generation blends with existing variability. When the PV system reduces your daytime grid import, this reduction is difficult to distinguish from periods when the generator is running and offsetting grid consumption. The smart meter sees reduced grid import — but cannot determine whether this is caused by PV generation, generator operation, or simply lower facility demand.
  3. Conservative PV sizing. The 60 kWp system is modestly sized relative to the total electrical demand of a family park operation. The load profile shift is not dramatic — it represents a partial reduction in daytime import, not a complete elimination.
  4. Combined effect. The combination of an existing generator, variable facility loads (seasonal, event-driven), and a conservatively sized PV system means that the consumption pattern at the smart meter does not produce a clear "PV generation signature" in the way that a residential installation with no other generation would.
