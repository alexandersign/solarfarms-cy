1. Executive Summary

Christos owns a 3.3 MWp solar park in Nicosia district. Of this, 1.7 MWp sits outside the grid connection terms — generating energy that cannot be exported and earns zero revenue. This proposal converts that stranded 1.7 MWp into Bitcoin mining revenue via a Solhash joint venture.

1.7 MWp × 2,500 kWh/kWp = 4,250 MWh/year of stranded solar energy. Containerised S21+ miners deployed on-site consume this energy at €0/kWh electricity cost. Revenue is split 30% to Christos (park) / 70% to the Mining Partner.

4. 30/70 Split Economics

The 30/70 split reflects capital at risk, asset lifecycle, and opportunity cost each party bears.

Christos — Park Partner (30%)

Provides: 1.7 MWp solar panels, inverters, land access.

Capital tied up: €1,275K (pro-rated PV capex), earning €0 for 3–5 years without JV.

Receives: 30% of net → €204,114/yr (4.80¢/kWh) at BTC €100K.

5-year total: €1,021K — recovers ~83% of total pre-connection costs.

No additional investment. All equipment and EPC paid by Mining Partner.

Mining Partner (70%)

Invests: €1,053,160 (equipment + EPC).

Receives: 70% of net → €476,266/yr at BTC €100K.

Payback: 2.21 years. IRR: ~35%.

5-year profit: €1,328,170 (after capex recovery).

Exit: Equipment relocates to next park when grid connection comes.

Christos provides the scarce resource — a permitted, built solar site with panels, trackers, and inverters worth €1,275K (pro-rated). This asset retains >95% of its value and has a guaranteed future use (grid PPA). The 30% revenue share (~4.80¢/kWh at BTC €100K) compensates for tied-up capital while remaining well below the ~10¢ grid PPA rate.
Mining Partner takes the operational and market risk — BTC price, halving, network difficulty, and equipment obsolescence. Their €899K in ASICs depreciates to ~10% value over 5 years. The 70% share compensates for this consumable-asset risk with a target payback of 2.21 years.
