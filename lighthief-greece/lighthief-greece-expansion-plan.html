Lighthief Greece IKE

1. Executive Summary

Lighthief plans to establish a Greek subsidiary — Lighthief Greece IKE (Ιδιωτική Κεφαλαιουχική Εταιρεία) — to capture the rapidly expanding Greek BESS and energy storage market. Greece has opened a 4.7 GW battery storage pipeline valued at approximately €1.9 billion in hardware and installation, with regulatory support now firmly in place for projects commencing operation from 2026 onwards.

The Greek IKE (Private Capital Company) structure offers the lowest formation cost, full foreign ownership, limited liability, and digital incorporation — making it the ideal vehicle for market entry.

2. Why Greece, Why Now

Market Opportunity

  • 4.7 GW total BESS capacity allocated nationally (3,800 MW transmission + 900 MW distribution)
  • €1.9 billion estimated market volume for hardware and installation
  • Priority grid connection framework established (Ministerial Decision, March 2025)
  • RAAEY Decision E-65/2025 defines operational support methodology for storage stations from 1 January 2026
  • Diverse business models enabled: industrial coupling, RE aggregator integration, coal phase-out zones, decentralised distribution storage

3. Recommended Company Structure

Entity Type: IKE (Private Capital Company)

The IKE is the most popular structure for foreign investors in Greece due to:

  • Minimum capital: €1 (flexible contributions in cash, kind, or services)
  • Limited liability for all shareholders
  • No mandatory local shareholder — 100% foreign ownership permitted
  • Digital incorporation via e-One Stop Service (e-OSS) in 1–3 business days
  • No notary required when using model articles with no in-kind contributions

7. Incorporation Checklist

Pre-Incorporation

  • Confirm company name availability (GEMI search)
  • Decide on share capital amount (€1 minimum, suggest €1,000–€5,000 for credibility)
  • Appoint managing director(s) — can be the Cyprus parent's representative
  • Obtain Power of Attorney for remote incorporation (notarised & apostilled in Cyprus)
  • Engage Greek lawyer for articles of association review

Incorporation Steps

  • Register via e-One Stop Service (e-OSS) or physical One Stop Shop
  • Submit Articles of Association (model statute or custom)
  • Obtain GEMI registration number
  • Obtain Tax Identification Number (AFM) from local tax office (DOY)
  • Register with EFKA (social insurance fund)
  • Open corporate bank account (Alpha Bank, Eurobank, Piraeus, NBG)
  • Register for VAT (if annual turnover expected to exceed €10,000)
  • Set up myDATA / e-invoicing integration

Post-Incorporation

  • Appoint local accountant / bookkeeper
  • Obtain any sector-specific licences (energy sector permits, if applicable)
  • Register with local Chamber of Commerce
  • Set up payroll for local employees
  • Establish intercompany agreement with Lighthief Cyprus (transfer pricing)

8. Key Regulatory Considerations

TEE Registration (Mandatory for Tender Participation)

⚠ CRITICAL — TEE Registration is mandatory. Without a TEE-licensed engineer, the company cannot participate in BESS tenders, submit engineering designs to RAE/RAAEY, or sign off on grid connection applications.
  • TEE (Technical Chamber of Greece) membership is mandatory to sign off on engineering designs, technical studies, and tender submissions — equivalent of ETEK in Cyprus
  • The company must employ at least one TEE-registered engineer with an active practice licence (Άδεια Ασκήσεως Επαγγέλματος)
  • TEE registration requires: Greek polytechnic degree (or equivalent EU-recognised engineering degree) + TEE examinations (held 3–4 times per year)
  • Foreign engineers with EU qualifications can apply for TEE recognition under EU Directive 2005/36/EC (mutual recognition of professional qualifications)

BESS Project Requirements

For bidding on Greek storage projects:

  • TEE-licensed engineer on staff (see above)
  • Minimum 2-hour discharge capacity
  • Minimum 0.5 MW power output
  • ≥80% system efficiency
  • Valid storage permit from RAE/RAAEY
  • Financial solidity: priority bonds and pre-approved bank loans covering total CAPEX (reference cost €200/kWh)
  • Technical compliance with Greek grid code

Transfer Pricing

  • Intercompany transactions between Lighthief Cyprus and Lighthief Greece must be at arm's length
  • Prepare transfer pricing documentation to support any management fees, IP licensing, or procurement margin

EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive

0% withholding tax on dividends from Greece to Cyprus if:

  • Lighthief Cyprus holds ≥10% of Lighthief Greece IKE
  • Holding period ≥24 months
  • Both entities are EU tax residents

9. Strategic Recommendations

  1. Hire a TEE-licensed engineer first — This is the single most critical hire. Without TEE registration, Lighthief Greece cannot participate in tenders, sign engineering designs, or submit studies to RAE/RAAEY. This person should combine technical credentials with business development capability — effectively serving as Country Lead. Target profile: senior electrical engineer, 5–10 years energy/BESS experience, based in Athens, active TEE membership.
  2. Start lean (Scenario A) — Use the first 12 months for market development, relationship building, and securing initial project mandates before scaling headcount.
  3. Base in Athens — Proximity to RAE/RAAEY, ADMIE (grid operator), and major energy companies (METLEN, PPC, Terna Energy). Thessaloniki is a viable alternative if targeting Northern Greece / Macedonia projects (Western Macedonia has 425 MW BESS allocation).
  4. Leverage the Cyprus portfolio — Position Lighthief Greece as the natural extension of the 881 MWh Cyprus BESS portfolio, demonstrating track record and OEM relationships.
  5. Target coal phase-out zones — Western Macedonia (425 MW allocation) offers priority access and regional development incentives.
  6. Partner with local EPC firms initially rather than building a full Greek construction team — reduces fixed cost and risk.
  7. Engage a Greek energy lawyer early to navigate RAE permitting, grid connection applications, and PPA/offtake structures.
