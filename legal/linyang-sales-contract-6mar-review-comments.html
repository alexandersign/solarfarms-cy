Comment 1: Payment Terms — 30/60/10 Structure, Late Payment Framework & Default DISCUSSED ON CALL

Location: Article 6 — Terms of Payment

Issue: Payment milestone percentages are currently blank. As discussed on our call, Linyang has proposed a 30/60/10 structure. We understand Linyang's preference and appreciate the increased holdback at 10% (vs. the standard 5%). However, the current structure places 90% of payment before delivery, which creates significant cash flow exposure for the Buyer, particularly given Cyprus import VAT obligations. Additionally, the contract requires a comprehensive late payment framework governing interest, default triggers, and the consequences of non-payment — for both the Buyer-Seller and Buyer-Client relationships.

CONTEXT — CYPRUS VAT: Under Cyprus customs law, the Buyer (importer) pays 19% VAT on the CIF value at point of import before goods can be released. For a batch with CIF value of approximately EUR 28M, this represents approximately EUR 5.3M in VAT payable at customs clearance. With 90% already paid pre-shipment, the Buyer must finance both the final 10% payment and the VAT obligation simultaneously upon arrival.

Linyang Proposed Structure:

Advance30%Contract signing / PO
Pre-Shipment (FAT)60%Ex-Works ready / FAT passed

Lighthief Proposed Adjustment:

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: We would like to propose a slight adjustment to the milestone structure, shifting a portion of the pre-shipment payment to a post-delivery (DAP) milestone. This allows Lighthief to manage VAT cash flow without reducing Linyang's total pre-delivery exposure materially.
MilestoneLinyang (30/60/10)Lighthief ProposedTrigger
Advance30%30%Contract signing / PO — unchanged
Pre-Shipment (FAT)60%50%Factory inspection passed, ready to ship
Arrival (DAP)—10%CIF Limassol, customs cleared, goods on site
SAT / Holdback10%10%Commissioning + PAC signed
RATIONALE: Under this adjustment:
  • Linyang receives 80% before shipment (vs. 90% under 30/60/10) — a reduction of only 10%
  • The 10% DAP milestone is payable upon arrival and customs clearance — typically 4–6 weeks after Ex-Works
  • Linyang's total pre-delivery exposure reduces from 90% to 80%, which is still well above industry norms for CIF contracts
  • The holdback remains at 10% as Linyang proposed
  • The DAP milestone gives Lighthief a short window to arrange VAT financing against confirmed delivery
If the 30/50/10/10 adjustment is not acceptable, we are open to discussing 30/55/5/10 as a compromise — maintaining 85% pre-delivery while still creating a small post-arrival milestone for VAT management.

Comment 2: Affiliate Coverage — Anti-Circumvention Protection (Art. 1A) IMPORTANT DISCUSSED ON CALL

Location: Article 1A — Contractual Context

Issue: As discussed on our call, the current Art. 1A.3 text naming only Solarfun and Jiangsu Linyang is accepted as-is. However, the Sales Contract should include an explicit anti-circumvention clause to protect the commercial structure. Under the agreed model, the Buyer receives CIF-0 distributor pricing from the Seller and adds its own margins for end-customers. This structure only works if the Seller (and any related entity) cannot contract directly with end-customers introduced by the Buyer within the Territory.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: We accept Art. 1A.3 as currently drafted. In place of broader affiliate binding, we propose a new Art. 1A.4 anti-circumvention clause. This protects the distributor pricing model and is consistent with the exclusivity and client protection provisions in the Distribution Agreement.

Current Text (Art. 1A.3) — ACCEPTED:

For the avoidance of doubt, references to the Seller in this Contract include Solarfun Renewable Energy Poland sp. z o.o., Jiangsu Linyang Energy Storage Technology Co., Ltd.

Proposed New Clause — Insert as Art. 1A.4:

1A.4 Anti-Circumvention: The Seller shall not, and shall procure that its Affiliates, subsidiaries, parent companies, and any related entities shall not, directly or indirectly: (a) enter into any sales, supply, distribution, service, or maintenance contract with any end-customer introduced by the Buyer within the Territory (Republic of Cyprus); (b) solicit, approach, or accept orders from any end-customer with whom the Buyer has an existing or pending commercial relationship within the Territory; or (c) supply Commodities or services to any third party for resale or installation within the Territory, where such supply would circumvent the Buyer's exclusive distribution rights under the Distribution Agreement. This obligation shall survive termination of this Contract for a period of two (2) years and is without prejudice to the broader exclusivity and client protection provisions in the Distribution Agreement, which shall prevail in the event of any conflict.
COMMERCIAL NOTE: This clause protects the agreed commercial model: Linyang provides equipment at CIF-0 distributor pricing; Lighthief adds margins and manages end-customer relationships in Cyprus. The anti-circumvention clause ensures neither party's commercial interests are undermined by direct approaches to introduced clients. The full exclusivity and territory protection is governed by the Distribution Agreement — this clause provides a specific, enforceable safeguard within the Sales Contract itself.

Issue: Several items in the Seller's installation scope appear incomplete. Item (a) is blank, and items relating to installation supervision and workmanship warranty are absent. For a contract where the Seller is responsible for OEM installation and commissioning, the scope must be clearly defined to avoid disputes during site works.

Current Text:

(a) ; (b) OEM commissioning and system configuration; (c) Connection of battery modules, PCS, BMS, and cooling systems; (d) OEM testing protocols and verification;

Proposed Replacement:

(a) Equipment installation and assembly by factory-trained technicians; (b) OEM commissioning and system configuration; (c) Connection of battery modules, PCS, BMS, and cooling systems; (d) OEM testing protocols and verification; (e) Installation supervision and quality assurance; (f) Installation workmanship warranty (5 years from PAC).
LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: Item (f) is particularly important — the Seller is performing installation and commissioning, so a workmanship warranty on that scope is standard practice. Without this, any installation defect would fall into a gap between the product warranty (which covers manufacturing) and the Buyer's EPC scope (which does not include OEM installation).

Comment 4: No Cross-Liability — Restore (Art. 5C) CRITICAL

Comment 5: Late Payment Framework & End-Customer Pass-Through (Art. 6) IMPORTANT DISCUSSED ON CALL

Location: Article 6 — Terms of Payment (existing "no default" sub-clause)

Issue: The contract includes a clause stating the Buyer shall not be deemed in default due to end-customer payment delays. As discussed on our call, Linyang does not accept automatic deadline extensions for late end-customer payments. We accept this position. Instead, we propose amending the existing clause to align with the late payment framework set out in Comment 1 — under which late payments (regardless of cause) incur EU statutory interest, the delivery schedule extends day-for-day, and non-payment beyond 60 days triggers default.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: The existing "no default" clause should be replaced with the structured late payment framework agreed on our call. This gives Linyang certainty (interest accrues from day one of any late payment) while giving Lighthief a clear mechanism to pass through the same charges to the end-customer.

Current Text:

The Buyer shall not be deemed in default, nor shall shipment suspension, interest, penalties, or termination rights arise, due solely to delays in end-customer payment beyond the Buyer's reasonable control.

Proposed Replacement:

Late Payment and End-Customer Pass-Through: (a) The Buyer's payment obligations under this Contract are absolute and are not conditional upon the Buyer's receipt of payment from any end-customer. (b) Where the Buyer fails to make any payment under Article 6(b)–(d) by the due date, interest shall accrue at the rate prescribed by EU Directive 2011/7/EU (ECB main refinancing rate + 8% per annum, simple interest), regardless of whether the delay is caused by the Buyer or by the Buyer's end-customer. (c) Where late payment occurs, the Seller's delivery and commissioning obligations shall be extended day-for-day by the period of such late payment, without penalty to the Seller. (d) If any payment under Article 6(b)–(d) remains unpaid for more than sixty (60) calendar days after the due date, the Seller may terminate this Contract in accordance with Article 6(g). (e) The Buyer shall include equivalent late payment and default provisions in its downstream contracts with end-customers, ensuring that EU Directive 2011/7/EU statutory interest rates and sixty (60) day default triggers are mirrored in the Buyer-Client agreement.
NOTE: This framework cross-references the late payment and default provisions in Comment 1 (Article 6(e)–(h)). Both sets of provisions should be read together. The intention is that Articles 6(e)–(h) set out the mechanics, while this clause clarifies the treatment of end-customer-caused delays and the pass-through obligation.

Comment 6: Late Payment Interest Rate (Art. 6) IMPORTANT DISCUSSED ON CALL

Location: Article 6 — Cyprus interest rate sub-clause

Issue: The contract specifies 2% per month, compounded monthly for Cyprus contracts. This equates to an effective annual rate of approximately 26.8%, which is commercially punitive and may be unenforceable under Cyprus law. As discussed on our call and set out in Comments 1 and 5, all late payment interest under this contract should follow the EU Directive 2011/7/EU statutory rate. This provides a single, consistent, legally defensible rate across the entire contract.

Current Text:

For contracts where the end destination is Cyprus, the interest rate shall be 2% per month, compounded monthly, as per Cyprus commercial practice.

Proposed Replacement:

For contracts where the end destination is Cyprus, the interest rate on late payments shall be the rate prescribed by EU Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment in commercial transactions — being the European Central Bank (ECB) main refinancing rate plus eight percent (8%) per annum, calculated on a simple interest basis. This rate shall apply to all late payment obligations under this Article 6 and shall be the sole interest rate applicable to overdue amounts.
NOTE: The EU Directive 2011/7/EU rate is currently approximately 12% per annum (ECB rate ~4% + 8%). This is commercially firm — significantly higher than standard commercial lending rates — while being legally defensible and consistent across all EU/EEA jurisdictions. Using this rate also ensures consistency with the late payment framework in Articles 6(e)–(h) and the end-customer pass-through in Comment 5, where the same rate applies throughout the contractual chain.

Comment 8: INSERT — Product Performance Warranties (New Section 8D) CRITICAL INSERT DISCUSSED ON CALL

Location: After Article 8C (LTSA Separation) — insert as new Section 8D

Issue: As discussed on our call, we are merging the guarantee framework into this contract. The contract currently contains a 5-year product warranty (Art. 8) and correctly separates LTSA-governed service commitments (Art. 8C). However, the product's inherent performance characteristics — State of Health, Round-Trip Efficiency, cycle life, and PCS efficiency — are not warranted anywhere in the contract. These are OEM product specifications, not service-level commitments, and must be warranted as product characteristics independent of any LTSA.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: Without binding performance specifications, the Buyer has no contractual basis to hold the Seller accountable for product performance. The Seller's warranty (Art. 8) covers defects in "design, material or workmanship" but does not define what constitutes acceptable performance. Section 8C explicitly excludes performance guarantees from this contract unless an LTSA exists — but product-level specifications are distinct from service-level guarantees and should be warranted here.

Proposed New Section — Insert as "Section 8D — PRODUCT PERFORMANCE WARRANTIES":

8D.1 State of Health (SOH) Guarantees: The Seller warrants that the battery cells and modules, when operated in accordance with the Seller's guidelines (1 cycle/day average, 90% DoD, 25±2°C), shall retain the following minimum State of Health: (a) Year 5: ≥ 85% SOH; (b) Year 10: ≥ 79.58% SOH; (c) Year 15: ≥ 70% SOH. SOH remedy: The Seller shall supply replacement cells/modules (FOB or CIF Limassol as applicable) to restore capacity above the guaranteed threshold. Replacement parts are at Seller's cost; installation labour is at Buyer's cost unless an LTSA provides otherwise. 8D.2 Round-Trip Efficiency (RTE): The Seller warrants that the full system AC-AC Round-Trip Efficiency, including PCS conversion, auxiliary consumption, and internal DC/AC cabling losses within the Seller's supply scope, shall be not less than 86.32% at rated power under standard test conditions (0.5C charge / 0.5C discharge, 25±2°C). This warranty is conditional upon the Buyer's external MV cabling (from MV Skid to Point of Common Coupling) conforming to the cable specifications and maximum allowable loss parameters set out in the Seller's system design drawings. The Seller shall provide the Buyer with the applicable cable loss budget and specifications within fourteen (14) days of contract signing. Any RTE shortfall attributable solely to Buyer-supplied cables exceeding the specified loss ratings shall not constitute a breach of this warranty. 8D.3 Cycle Life: The Seller warrants a minimum cycle life of 7,000 equivalent full cycles at a standard charge/discharge rate of 0.5C/0.5C, at 90% Depth of Discharge (DoD) to 70% End of Life (EOL), at 25±2°C, under the operating conditions specified in the Seller's Warranty Terms (Version 2). For the avoidance of doubt, the maximum charge/discharge rate is 1C. Operation at rates exceeding 0.5C may affect achievable cycle life and shall be governed by the degradation curves provided in the Seller's Warranty Terms V2. 8D.4 PCS Efficiency: The Seller warrants PCS conversion efficiency of not less than 98% at rated power. 8D.5 Remedies: If the Products fail to meet the performance thresholds in §8D.1–8D.4, the Seller's obligation shall be limited to repair or replacement of defective components in accordance with Article 8 (Warranty). For the avoidance of doubt, availability LDs and revenue-based compensation remain governed exclusively by any applicable LTSA under Article 8C. 8D.6 Reference Documents: The following Seller documents form part of the performance warranty and are incorporated by reference: (a) Warranty Terms — Version 2; (b) Battery Container Specification (3.34 MWh); (c) PCS Datasheets (BCS-C-HUD series); (d) RTE Calculation Report (including cable loss budget assumptions).

Comment 10: INSERT — Delay Liquidated Damages (New Section 9C) CRITICAL INSERT

Location: After new Section 9B — insert as Section 9C

Issue: The contract contains no financial penalty for late delivery. For a contract of this value, with downstream client commitments that carry their own delay penalties, a delivery delay framework is essential. Without this, Lighthief bears the full financial risk of any Seller delay with no recourse.

Proposed New Section — Insert as "Section 9C — DELIVERY DELAY LIQUIDATED DAMAGES":

9C.0 Definitions for LD Purposes: "Critical Path Equipment" — Equipment whose delay prevents commissioning of the entire order or park. Includes: Power Conversion System (PCS), Medium-Voltage Switchgear/Skid (MV Skid), and main step-up transformer. Delay to any Critical Path Equipment constitutes a Full Order Delay. "Non-Critical Path Equipment" — Equipment whose delay prevents commissioning of only a portion of the total ordered capacity. Battery containers (BESS units) are Non-Critical Path Equipment when delivered individually as part of a multi-container order, provided the PCS and MV infrastructure are available to commission the remaining containers. "Full Order Delay" — Delay caused by late delivery, defect, or unavailability of any Critical Path Equipment, resulting in no portion of the order being capable of commissioning. "Partial Delay" — Delay caused by late delivery, defect, or unavailability of Non-Critical Path Equipment, where the remaining equipment is capable of commissioning at reduced capacity. "Affected Capacity" — The proportion of total ordered energy capacity (MWh) that cannot be commissioned due to the delayed equipment, expressed as a percentage of total capacity. "LD Value" — For a Full Order Delay: the total order value (all goods). For a Partial Delay: the value of the delayed goods only (= order value × Affected Capacity %). 9C.1 Delay Liquidated Damages: (a) Days 1–30 of delay: 0.1% of LD Value per day; (b) Days 31–60 of delay: 0.15% of LD Value per day; (c) Days 61+ of delay: 0.2% of LD Value per day; (d) Cap: aggregate delay LDs shall not exceed 10% of LD Value per order. 9C.2 Application: (a) PCS or MV Skid delayed → Full Order Delay → LD Value = total order value. (b) Individual battery container delayed (others can commission) → Partial Delay → LD Value = value of delayed container(s) only. (c) Delay LDs begin on the day following the agreed delivery date and accrue daily until actual delivery or termination. 9C.3 Exclusions: Delay LDs shall not apply where the delay is caused by: (a) Buyer's failure to pay on time; (b) Force Majeure under Article 15; (c) Buyer's failure to provide site readiness as required. 9C.4 Total Combined LD Cap: The aggregate of all liquidated damages under this Contract (delay + availability, if applicable) shall not exceed ten percent (10%) of the total contract value.

Comment 11: Liability Cap — Restore to 100% (Art. 9) CRITICAL

Location: Article 9 — Limitation of Liability

Issue: The general liability cap is set at 10% of total charges. On a EUR 85M+ contract, this limits recovery to approximately EUR 8.5M regardless of actual damage. This is commercially inadequate for a contract of this magnitude. The 10% cap is appropriate for warranty-specific claims (already addressed in Art. 8B), but the general liability cap for all breaches should reflect the full contract value.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: We accept the 10% cap for warranty services under Article 8B. However, the general liability cap covering all breaches, including non-delivery, fundamental breach, and indemnity obligations, must be restored to 100% of the total price paid. A 10% general cap does not provide adequate protection and is below market standard for equipment supply contracts of this value.

Current Text:

Subject to other clauses in this section, the total liability of the SELLER of all breaches of duty under this Contract shall not in any event or under any circumstances, exceed ten percent of total charges.

Proposed Replacement:

Subject to other clauses in this section, the total aggregate liability of the SELLER for all breaches of duty under this Contract shall not exceed one hundred percent (100%) of the total price paid by the BUYER to the SELLER for the Products giving rise to the claim. For the avoidance of doubt, this general liability cap is without prejudice to the warranty-specific liability cap in Section 8B, which shall apply to warranty claims independently.
ALTERNATIVE: If 100% is not acceptable, we would consider a tiered approach:
  • 10% for warranty claims (Art. 8B — already agreed)
  • 50% for general contractual breaches
  • 100% for fraud, wilful misconduct, and manufacturing defect indemnities (Art. 9A carve-out)

Comment 13: Force Majeure — Complete Cure Mechanism (Art. 15) IMPORTANT

Location: Article 15 — Force Majeure, Buyer Cure Right sub-section

Issue: The contract includes a "Buyer Cure Right and Deposit Return" heading but the cure period is blank and the deposit refund mechanism is incomplete. The section currently reads: "If a Force Majeure event affecting the Seller's delivery obligations continues for a period exceeding () days," with no further provisions.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: The Force Majeure cure right needs to be completed. Without a defined cure period and refund mechanism, the Buyer has no exit from a prolonged Force Majeure situation and no way to recover advance payments.

Proposed Completion — Replace existing Buyer Cure Right text:

Buyer Cure Right and Deposit Return: (a) If a Force Majeure event affecting the Seller's delivery obligations continues for a period exceeding sixty (60) days, the Buyer may, upon written notice, cancel the affected order without liability. (b) Upon such cancellation, the Seller shall refund the Buyer's advance payment within thirty (30) days, less any documented costs for materials already procured and non-recoverable. (c) The Seller shall provide reasonable evidence of claimed non-recoverable costs within fourteen (14) days of the cancellation notice.
