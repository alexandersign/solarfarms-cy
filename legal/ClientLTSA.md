3. DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION
Unless otherwise defined herein, capitalised terms shall have the meanings given to them in the EPC Agreement.
For the purposes of this Agreement:
"BESS"
 means the battery energy storage system installed at the Site, as described in Schedule 1.
"Business Day"
 means a day other than a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday in the Republic of Cyprus.
"Commissioning Date"
 means the date of issuance of the Provisional Acceptance Certificate (PAC) under the EPC Agreement.
"Critical Alert"
 means any alert indicating a condition that requires immediate attention to prevent system failure, safety hazard, or significant performance degradation.
"EMS"
 means Energy Management System, which is a third-party system unless expressly supplied by the Service Provider.
"Maintenance Visit"
 means a scheduled on-site visit by the Service Provider's qualified personnel to perform preventive maintenance activities.
"Monitoring Platform"
 means the remote monitoring software and infrastructure used by the Service Provider to collect, analyse, and display BESS performance data.
"Performance Data"
 means data collected from the BESS including but not limited to power output, energy throughput, state of charge (SOC), state of health (SOH), temperature, voltage, current, and alarm logs.
"Preventive Maintenance"
 means scheduled maintenance activities designed to prevent equipment failure and ensure optimal performance.
"Response Time"
 means the time between the Service Provider receiving notification of an issue and the commencement of diagnostic or remedial action.
"Service Fee"
 means the annual fee payable by the Client for the services under this Agreement, as set out in Schedule 2.
"Site"
 means the location where the BESS is installed, as specified in Schedule 1.
"SOC"
 means State of Charge, expressed as a percentage of remaining battery capacity.
"SOH"
 means State of Health, expressed as a percentage of the battery's original rated capacity.
"Availability"
 means the percentage of time during which the BESS is capable of operating at its rated capacity, calculated as set out in Schedule 4.
"Availability Guarantee"
 means the minimum annual Availability level guaranteed by the Service Provider, as set out in Schedule 4.
"Corrective Maintenance"
 means unscheduled maintenance activities required to restore the BESS to operational condition following a fault or failure.
"Degradation Curve"
 means the expected reduction in battery capacity over time under normal operating conditions, as set out in Schedule 5.
"Guaranteed SOH"
 means the minimum State of Health guaranteed by the Service Provider at any given point during the Term, as defined in Schedule 5.
"Liquidated Damages"
 means the predetermined damages payable by the Service Provider in the event of failure to meet guaranteed performance levels.
"Scheduled Downtime"
 means planned maintenance periods agreed in advance, which shall not exceed ten (10) days per calendar year.
"System Capacity"
 means the total installed capacity of the BESS in MWh, as specified in Schedule 1.

5. REMOTE MONITORING SERVICES
5.1 Monitoring Coverage
 The Service Provider shall provide remote monitoring of the BESS on a 24/7 basis, 365 days per year, via the Monitoring Platform.
5.2 Monitored Parameters
 The Monitoring Platform shall collect and analyse the following Performance Data:
 (a) System power output (kW) and energy throughput (kWh);
 (b) Battery state of charge (SOC) and state of health (SOH);
 (c) Cell-level and module-level voltage and temperature;
 (d) Power Conversion System (PCS) performance and efficiency;
 (e) Ambient temperature and environmental conditions;
 (f) Alarm and fault logs;
 (g) Communication status and connectivity;
 (h) Inverter and transformer status.
5.3 Alert Classification
 Alerts shall be classified as follows:
 (a) Critical Alert: Immediate risk to safety, equipment, or system availability. Response within four (4) hours.
 (b) Major Alert: Significant degradation in performance or reliability. Response within twenty-four (24) hours.
 (c) Minor Alert: Non-urgent issues requiring attention during next scheduled maintenance. Response within seventy-two (72) hours.
 (d) Informational: Data logging and trend analysis. No immediate response required.
5.4 Alert Notification
 (a) Critical Alerts shall be notified to the Client by telephone and email within thirty (30) minutes of detection.
 (b) Major Alerts shall be notified to the Client by email within two (2) hours of detection.
 (c) Minor and Informational Alerts shall be included in the next scheduled report.
5.5 Client Portal Access
 The Service Provider shall provide the Client with secure access to the Monitoring Platform, enabling the Client to view real-time and historical Performance Data.
5.6 Reporting
 The Service Provider shall provide the following reports:
 (a) Monthly Performance Report: Summary of energy throughput, availability, SOC/SOH trends, alerts, and key performance indicators. Delivered within ten (10) Business Days of month end.
 (b) Quarterly Health Report: Detailed analysis of battery health, degradation trends, thermal performance, and recommendations. Delivered within fifteen (15) Business Days of quarter end.
 (c) Annual Review Report: Comprehensive annual assessment including performance benchmarking, maintenance summary, and forward recommendations. Delivered within thirty (30) days of each anniversary of the Commissioning Date.
5.7 Data Retention
 The Service Provider shall retain Performance Data for a minimum of five (5) years from the date of collection.
5.8 Data Ownership
 All Performance Data collected from the BESS shall be owned by the Client. The Service Provider shall have a licence to use such data for the purposes of providing services under this Agreement and for anonymised benchmarking and product improvement.

6. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE
6.1 OEM Compliance
 All maintenance activities shall be performed in accordance with the OEM Maintenance Manual (Linyang Power Atlantic Battery Container Maintenance Manual) and applicable OEM guidelines. The Service Provider shall not perform any maintenance operations beyond those specified in the OEM manual without prior OEM authorisation.
6.2 Maintenance Schedule
 The Service Provider shall perform preventive maintenance in accordance with the following schedule:
 (a) Monthly Remote Checks: Container status monitoring, BMS data backup, parameter review, and software update assessment as required by OEM.
 (b) Bi-Annual On-Site Maintenance Visits: Comprehensive on-site inspection and maintenance by qualified personnel, conducted every six (6) months (two visits per year).
 (c) Quarterly Remote Health Check: Remote diagnostic review of all monitored parameters, SOH assessment, and system optimisation recommendations.
 (d) Annual Grounding Test: Verification of ground connection with resistance measurement (must not exceed 4Ω per OEM specification).
 (e) Additional visits as required based on monitoring alerts, OEM recommendations, or Corrective Maintenance needs.
6.3 OEM-Mandated Maintenance Items
 The following items are mandatory per OEM specification:
 (a) Monthly: Container condition check, BMS data download and backup, run data/parameter/log saving, software update review;
 (b) Every Six Months: Cable shielding inspection, surge protection device (SPD) verification, fuse inspection, copper bus bar check;
 (c) Annually: Cable layout inspection, ground resistance test (≤4Ω), equipotential bonding verification, cable torque verification per OEM specification.
6.4 Weather Restrictions
 Per OEM requirements, the Service Provider shall not open container doors for maintenance during rainy, humid, snowy, foggy, or excessively windy conditions. Maintenance visits shall be rescheduled if weather conditions are unsuitable.
6.5 Bi-Annual Maintenance Visit Scope
 Each Maintenance Visit shall include, at a minimum:
 (a) Visual inspection of all BESS components, enclosures, and connections;
 (b) Thermal imaging of electrical connections and switchgear;
 (c) Verification of battery module balancing and calibration;
 (d) Inspection and cleaning of cooling systems (fans, filters, HVAC);
 (e) Verification of fire suppression system status;
 (f) Inspection of cable terminations and torque verification;
 (g) Review and update of firmware and software as recommended by OEM;
 (h) Functional testing of protection relays and safety systems;
 (i) Calibration of sensors and metering equipment;
 (j) Review of alarm and event logs;
 (k) Documentation and reporting of findings;

 **CYPRUS COASTAL PROTECTION (C5 Enclosure Maintenance):**
 (l) Inspection of C5-rated coating integrity on enclosures, PCS, and switchgear;
 (m) Salt-deposit removal from HVAC intakes, ventilation grilles, and cooling air paths;
 (n) Inspection of door seals and gaskets for salt intrusion;
 (o) Verification of drainage channels and moisture egress paths;
 (p) Cleaning of external surfaces and removal of salt crystallisation;
 (q) Application of protective lubricant to door hinges, latches, and exposed fasteners;
 (r) Photographic documentation of coating condition for warranty records.
6.3 Quarterly Remote Health Check Scope
 Each quarterly remote health check shall include:
 (a) Analysis of SOH trends and degradation patterns;
 (b) Review of thermal performance and cooling efficiency;
 (c) Assessment of cell imbalance and module performance;
 (d) Verification of communication and data integrity;
 (e) Recommendations for operational adjustments;
 (f) Written summary report to the Client.
6.4 Maintenance Scheduling
 (a) Bi-Annual Maintenance Visits shall be scheduled at least thirty (30) days in advance, at mutually agreed dates approximately six (6) months apart.
 (b) The Service Provider shall endeavour to schedule maintenance at times when the BESS is not actively dispatching, to minimise impact on Client operations.
 (c) Total Scheduled Downtime shall not exceed ten (10) days per calendar year, distributed across preventive maintenance visits, OEM service windows, and system health checks.
 (d) The Client shall ensure site access and availability of relevant personnel during scheduled visits.
 (e) If the Client cancels or postpones a scheduled visit with less than fourteen (14) days' notice, reasonable rebooking costs may apply.
6.5 Maintenance Records
 The Service Provider shall maintain detailed records of all maintenance activities, findings, and recommendations, and shall provide copies to the Client upon request.
6.6 OEM Coordination
 Where maintenance activities require OEM involvement or specialised OEM parts, the Service Provider shall coordinate with the OEM on behalf of the Client.

8. RESPONSE TIMES AND SERVICE LEVELS

8.1 24/7 Monitoring Capability
 The Service Provider shall maintain the following monitoring infrastructure:
 (a) **Automated Alert System:** The Monitoring Platform shall automatically generate alerts based on predefined thresholds without requiring manual intervention;
 (b) **Multi-Channel Notification:** Critical Alerts shall trigger immediate notifications via:
  (i) SMS to duty technician's mobile phone;
  (ii) Email to duty technician and operations manager;
  (iii) Push notification via monitoring app (where available).
 (c) **Duty Roster:** The Service Provider shall maintain a 24/7 duty roster with at least one qualified technician on-call at all times;
 (d) **Escalation Protocol:** If the primary duty technician does not acknowledge a Critical Alert within thirty (30) minutes, the system shall automatically escalate to the backup technician and operations manager.

8.2 Response Time Commitments
 The Service Provider commits to the following Response Times:
 (a) Critical Alerts: Initial remote response within two (2) hours; on-site attendance (if required) within eight (8) hours.
 (b) Major Alerts: Initial remote response within eight (8) hours; on-site attendance (if required) within forty-eight (48) hours.
 (c) Minor Alerts: Initial remote response within forty-eight (48) hours; on-site attendance (if required) at the next scheduled maintenance visit or within five (5) business days, whichever is sooner.
 (d) **Response Time Measurement:** Response Time is measured from the timestamp of alert generation in the Monitoring Platform, not from Client notification.
 (e) **Clarification — Response vs Resolution:** The Response Times set out above apply to initial response and on-site attendance only. Resolution time depends on fault severity, spare parts availability, and OEM support requirements, and is not subject to the Response Time commitments in this Section.

8.3 Response Scope
 Initial response shall include:
 (a) Acknowledgement of the issue;
 (b) Remote diagnosis where possible;
 (c) Preliminary assessment and recommended actions;
 (d) Escalation to OEM if required.
8.3 On-Site Attendance
 Where on-site attendance is required:
 (a) The Service Provider shall dispatch qualified personnel to the Site;
 (b) Travel time shall not count toward Response Time;
 (c) The Client shall ensure site access and cooperation.
8.4 Exclusions from SLA
 Response Time commitments shall not apply to:
 (a) Issues caused by Client actions or third parties;
 (b) Force Majeure events;
 (c) Issues related to grid or external infrastructure;
 (d) Scheduled downtime or maintenance windows.
8.5 Service Level Credits
 (a) **Critical Alert Breach:** Where the Service Provider fails to achieve on-site attendance within the eight (8) hour commitment for a Critical Alert, the Client shall be entitled to a service credit of one hundred euros (€100) for each full hour of delay beyond the eight (8) hour commitment, up to a maximum of one thousand euros (€1,000) per incident.
 (b) **Major Alert Breach:** Where the Service Provider fails to achieve on-site attendance within the forty-eight (48) hour commitment for a Major Alert, the Client shall be entitled to a service credit of fifty euros (€50) for each full hour of delay beyond the forty-eight (48) hour commitment, up to a maximum of five hundred euros (€500) per incident.
 (c) **Quarterly Cap:** The total Service Level Credits payable under this Section 8.5 in any calendar quarter shall not exceed ten percent (10%) of the quarterly Service Fee for that quarter.
 (d) **Remediation Plan:** Where three (3) or more Service Level breaches occur in any calendar quarter, the Client may request a written remediation plan from the Service Provider. The Service Provider shall deliver such plan within fourteen (14) days of the request, detailing root cause analysis and corrective actions.
 (e) **Sole Remedy:** The Service Level Credits set out in this Section 8.5 shall be the Client's sole and exclusive remedy for failure to meet the Response Time commitments, and the Client shall not be entitled to claim additional damages for Response Time breaches beyond the Service Level Credits.

8.6 Escalation Protocol
 The Service Provider shall maintain the following escalation procedure for Critical Alerts:
 (a) **Acknowledgement:** All Critical Alerts shall be acknowledged by the duty technician within thirty (30) minutes of alert generation.
 (b) **Auto-Escalation:** If the primary duty technician does not acknowledge a Critical Alert within thirty (30) minutes, the Monitoring Platform shall automatically escalate the alert to the backup technician and the operations manager simultaneously.
 (c) **Management Escalation:** If on-site attendance has not been dispatched within four (4) hours of a Critical Alert, the operations manager shall escalate to the Service Provider's senior management and notify the Client's designated contact.
 (d) **Client Notification:** The Client shall receive status updates at intervals of no more than two (2) hours from the time of a Critical Alert until the issue is resolved or the situation is stabilised.

8.7 Coverage Hours
 (a) The Response Time commitments set out in Section 8.2 for Critical Alerts and Major Alerts apply on a **twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, three hundred and sixty-five days a year (24/7/365)** basis, including weekends, public holidays, and night hours.
 (b) The Response Time commitment for Minor Alerts applies during **business hours** (Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00 local time), excluding public holidays. Where a Minor Alert is raised outside business hours, the Response Time shall commence at the start of the next business day.
 (c) The Service Provider shall maintain a staffed duty roster at all times sufficient to meet the 24/7/365 commitment for Critical and Major Alerts, with a minimum of one (1) qualified technician on-call and a designated backup at all times.

9. AVAILABILITY GUARANTEE (TIER C AND D ONLY)
9.1 Availability Commitment
 Where the Client has selected Tier C or Tier C services, the Service Provider guarantees that the BESS shall achieve a minimum annual Availability of ninety-seven percent (97%), calculated in accordance with Schedule 4.
9.2 Calculation of Availability
 Availability shall be calculated as follows:
 Availability (%) = [(Total Hours in Year - Unavailable Hours) / Total Hours in Year] x 100
 Where:
 (a) Total Hours in Year = 8,760 hours (or 8,784 in a leap year);
 (b) Unavailable Hours = hours during which the BESS is unable to operate at its rated capacity due to equipment fault, failure, or Service Provider-caused downtime.
9.3 Exclusions from Unavailable Hours
 The following shall not count as Unavailable Hours:
 (a) Scheduled Downtime for preventive maintenance (maximum 240 hours / 10 days per year);
 (b) Downtime caused by grid unavailability, curtailment, or grid operator instruction;
 (c) Downtime caused by Force Majeure events;
 (d) Downtime caused by Client actions, third parties, or unauthorised modifications;
 (e) Downtime caused by EMS or third-party system failures;
 (f) Periods during which the BESS is not dispatched by the Client or market operator.
9.4 Availability Liquidated Damages
 If the annual Availability falls below 97%, the Service Provider shall pay Liquidated Damages to the Client as follows:
 (a) Availability 95% to < 97%: Five percent (5%) reduction in annual Service Fee;
 (b) Availability 93% to < 95%: Ten percent (10%) reduction in annual Service Fee;
 (c) Availability 90% to < 93%: Fifteen percent (15%) reduction in annual Service Fee;
 (d) Availability below 90%: Twenty percent (20%) reduction in annual Service Fee.
9.5 Cap on Availability Liquidated Damages
 The maximum Availability Liquidated Damages payable in any year shall not exceed twenty percent (20%) of the annual Service Fee.
9.6 Sole Remedy
 The Availability Liquidated Damages set out in this Section shall be the Client's sole and exclusive remedy for failure to achieve the Availability Guarantee, and the Client shall not be entitled to claim additional damages for unavailability beyond the Liquidated Damages.
9.7 Enhanced Availability Option
 Subject to separate written agreement and additional fees, the Service Provider may offer an enhanced Availability Guarantee of ninety-eight percent (98%) or higher, with adjusted Liquidated Damages as agreed between the Parties.

10. STATE OF HEALTH (SOH) AND PERFORMANCE WARRANTY (TIER D ONLY)

10.1 SOH Guarantee — OEM Pass-Through
 (a) Where the Client has selected Tier C services, the Service Provider guarantees that the BESS shall maintain a minimum State of Health in accordance with the Degradation Curve set out in Schedule 5.
 (b) **OEM-Backed Guarantee:** The SOH Guarantee is a pass-through guarantee provided by the OEM (Linyang) and is backed by the OEM's warranty reserve. The Service Provider's obligation under this Section is to facilitate the OEM's warranty and ensure proper maintenance is performed.
 (c) **Manufacturing Defect Determination:** Where SOH underperformance is attributable to a manufacturing defect or design flaw in the OEM equipment, the OEM's determination of the defect shall govern. The Service Provider shall facilitate the warranty claim process with the OEM.
 (d) **No Double Liability:** The Service Provider shall not be liable under both this Section 10 and Section 14.5 for the same underlying issue. Where SOH underperformance is caused by an OEM manufacturing defect, the remedy shall be through the OEM warranty, not through the Service Provider's direct liability.
10.2 Guaranteed SOH Levels
 The Guaranteed SOH at the end of each contract year shall be as follows (subject to adjustment based on actual cycling and operating conditions):
 (a) End of Year 1: 98% of original rated capacity;
 (b) End of Year 2: 96% of original rated capacity;
 (c) End of Year 3: 94% of original rated capacity;
 (d) End of Year 4: 92% of original rated capacity;
 (e) End of Year 5: 85% of original rated capacity;
 (f) End of Year 10: 79.58% of original rated capacity;
 (g) End of Year 15: 70% of original rated capacity.
 **Note:** The maximum SOH Guarantee period available from the OEM is fifteen (15) years. SOH Guarantees beyond Year 15 are not available. The above values are based on 1 cycle per day operation and are subject to the OEM's confirmed degradation profile.
10.3 SOH Measurement
 (a) SOH shall be measured at least annually using OEM-approved testing protocols.
 (b) The Service Provider shall provide the Client with a written SOH certificate following each annual measurement.
 (c) The Client may request additional SOH measurements at the Client's cost.
10.4 Remedies for SOH Underperformance
 If the measured SOH falls below the Guaranteed SOH, the Service Provider shall provide the following tiered remedies:

 (a) ISOLATED MODULE FAILURES (affecting less than 5% of total capacity):
  (i) The Service Provider shall replace or repair the affected modules at no cost to the Client;
  (ii) Replacement shall occur within sixty (60) days of identification;
  (iii) Replacement modules shall be new or equivalent to new in performance;
  (iv) **OEM Warranty:** Module replacements are sourced through the OEM warranty reserve where the failure is attributable to manufacturing factors. The OEM covers replacement components only; service fees, on-site replacement labor, and logistics costs are borne by the Service Provider and are included in the Tier C Service Fee.

 (b) SYSTEMIC UNDERPERFORMANCE (affecting 5% or more of total capacity):
  (i) The Service Provider shall, at its option, either:
   - Augment the BESS with additional cells to restore capacity to the Guaranteed SOH; or
   - Pay Liquidated Damages calculated as the cost to restore the system to Guaranteed SOH.
  (ii) If augmentation is chosen, it shall be completed within one hundred twenty (120) days;
  (iii) **OEM-Backed Augmentation:** The Service Provider's augmentation obligation is backed by a contractual commitment from the OEM to supply replacement cells under the OEM warranty reserve for SOH underperformance attributable to manufacturing factors or normal degradation exceeding the specified curve. The OEM covers replacement components only; service fees, on-site installation labor, and logistics costs are included in the Tier C Service Fee;
  (iv) Where augmentation cells are provided by the OEM under warranty, the Service Provider shall coordinate installation and commissioning;
  (v) Where SOH underperformance is caused by Client misuse, operational non-compliance, or factors excluded under Section 10.7, the augmentation obligation does not apply.

 (c) **OEM INSOLVENCY OR DEFAULT:**
  If the OEM fails to honour its warranty obligations (including due to insolvency, refusal, or cessation of business):
  (i) The Service Provider shall use commercially reasonable efforts to source equivalent replacement cells from alternative suppliers;
  (ii) The Service Provider's liability shall be limited to the caps set out in Section 10.5;
  (iii) The Service Provider may, at its option, terminate the Tier C service and refund a pro-rata portion of Tier C premiums paid;
  (iv) The Client shall have the right to downgrade to Tier C without penalty.
10.5 SOH Liquidated Damages Calculation
 Where Liquidated Damages are payable for SOH underperformance:
 (a) The Liquidated Damages shall equal the replacement cost of the capacity shortfall, calculated as:
  LD = (Guaranteed SOH - Actual SOH) x System Capacity x EUR [●] per kWh
 (b) The per-kWh rate shall be the prevailing market rate for equivalent battery modules at the time of claim;
 (c) The maximum SOH Liquidated Damages payable over the Term shall not exceed fifty percent (50%) of the total Service Fees paid.
10.6 OEM Warranty Reserve
 The SOH Guarantee is backed by the OEM's warranty reserve. The Service Provider shall ensure that adequate warranty provisions are maintained with the OEM to fulfil the obligations under this Section.
10.7 Exclusions from SOH Guarantee
 The SOH Guarantee shall not apply to capacity loss caused by:
 (a) Operation outside OEM-specified parameters;
 (b) Excessive cycling beyond design specifications;
 (c) Extreme temperature exposure beyond design limits;
 (d) Physical damage, vandalism, or Force Majeure;
 (e) Client modifications or third-party interventions not approved by the Service Provider.
10.8 Performance Testing
 (a) The Service Provider shall conduct annual performance tests to verify system capacity and efficiency.
 (b) Performance tests shall be conducted in accordance with IEC standards or OEM-specified protocols.
 (c) Test results shall be documented and provided to the Client within ten (10) Business Days of testing.

SOH MEASUREMENT PROTOCOL
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. SOH shall be measured annually using OEM-approved capacity test protocol
2. Test shall be conducted under controlled conditions:
   - Ambient temperature: 20-25°C
   - Full charge/discharge cycle at C/3 rate
   - Rest period of 2 hours between charge and discharge
3. Results shall be documented in SOH Certificate
4. Client may request additional tests at Client's cost

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REMEDIES FOR SOH UNDERPERFORMANCE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CATEGORY A: ISOLATED MODULE FAILURES
(Affecting < 5% of total system capacity)

Remedy: Module replacement at Service Provider's cost
Timeline: Within 60 days of identification
Standard: Replacement modules shall be new or equivalent to new

CATEGORY B: SYSTEMIC UNDERPERFORMANCE
(Affecting ≥ 5% of total system capacity)

Remedy Options (at Service Provider's election):
1. Cell Augmentation: Add cells to restore capacity to Guaranteed SOH
   - Timeline: Within 120 days
   - All costs borne by Service Provider

2. Liquidated Damages: Payment calculated as:
   LD = (Guaranteed SOH - Actual SOH) x System Capacity x Market Rate per kWh
   - Market Rate = prevailing cost of equivalent battery modules

Maximum SOH Liquidated Damages: 50% of total Service Fees paid over Term
