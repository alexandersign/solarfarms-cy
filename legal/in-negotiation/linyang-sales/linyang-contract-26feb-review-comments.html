Comment 1: Payment Terms — Renegotiation to 20/30/40/10 (VAT Optimised) CRITICAL

Location in New Contract: Article 6 — Terms of Payment, §6(a) and §6(b)

Issue: Payment milestone percentages are blank — the contract reads "% advance payment" and "() % payment" with no values.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST These percentages were defined in our amended sales agreement (§7.1) and confirmed in the Linyang RFI V1 Annex. They have been operating as agreed terms since January 2026.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: Payment milestones must be populated. Lighthief is proposing a revised payment structure of 20/30/40/10 (replacing the Distribution Agreement standard 25/50/20/5) in order to manage Cyprus import VAT cash flow obligations. The Buyer must pay 19% VAT at customs upon arrival of CIF goods in Limassol — the revised structure shifts more payment to post-delivery milestones, ensuring liquidity for VAT settlement without requiring a separate credit facility.

Current Text (New Contract §6):

(a) % advance payment by T/T within seven (7) days of contract effectiveness; () % payment by T/T prior to shipment of the Commodities from the manufacturing facility;

Proposed Replacement Text (REVISED — VAT-Optimised Structure):

(a) Twenty percent (20%) advance payment by T/T within seven (7) days of contract effectiveness or purchase order signing; (b) Thirty percent (30%) payment by T/T when the Products are ready for shipment (Ex-Works) from the manufacturing facility, upon Seller's written confirmation and photographic evidence of readiness; (c) Forty percent (40%) payment by T/T upon arrival of the Products on site (DAP — Delivered at Place, CIF Limassol) and completion of port clearance, following Buyer inspection and confirmation that the Commodities conform to the applicable Quotation; (d) Ten percent (10%) performance holdback by T/T upon successful completion of Site Acceptance Testing (SAT) and provisional acceptance of the Products on site.

Rationale for Revised Payment Terms:

COMMERCIAL NOTE: Under Cyprus customs law, the Buyer (importer) is required to pay 19% VAT on the CIF value at point of import. For a batch with CIF value of ~€28M, this represents ~€5.3M in VAT payable at customs clearance before goods can be released. The revised 20/30/40/10 structure reduces pre-shipment exposure from 75% to 50%, freeing working capital for VAT settlement. The higher DAP milestone (40% vs. 20%) is justified because by this point the Buyer has physical possession and the Seller's delivery obligation is substantially complete.

  • Compromise A: Accept 25/30/35/10 — preserves Linyang's 25% advance while still shifting weight to post-delivery.
  • Compromise B: Accept 25/35/30/10 — a more gradual shift that keeps the pre-shipment payment closer to 35% but still increases the holdback to 10%.

Comment 2: INSERT — Delay Liquidated Damages CRITICAL MISSING SECTION

Location in New Contract: Not present — should be inserted AFTER Article 8C (LTSA Separation) as new Section 9C

Issue: The entire Delay LD framework is absent. Without this section, Linyang faces zero financial penalty for late delivery. This was the most heavily negotiated section in our amendments, with definitions for Critical Path vs. Non-Critical Path equipment agreed through the RFI process.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST Fully drafted as Section 9C in our amended sales agreement (sales_agrement.md). Detailed negotiation history documented in linyang-sales-comments.html §9C.0–9C.1 with agreed compromise positions on LD Value basis.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: This is a critical omission. Section 9C — Liquidated Damages for Delay was previously negotiated and agreed in principle. The absence of any delivery delay penalty is commercially unacceptable for a €85M+ equipment supply contract. Please re-insert in full.

Proposed New Section — Insert as "Section 9C — DELIVERY DELAY LIQUIDATED DAMAGES":

9C.0 Definitions for LD Purposes: "Critical Path Equipment" — Equipment whose delay prevents commissioning of the entire order or park. Includes: Power Conversion System (PCS), Medium-Voltage Switchgear/Skid (MV Skid), and main step-up transformer. Delay to any Critical Path Equipment constitutes a Full Order Delay. "Non-Critical Path Equipment" — Equipment whose delay prevents commissioning of only a portion of the total ordered capacity. Battery containers (BESS units) are Non-Critical Path Equipment when delivered individually as part of a multi-container order, provided the PCS and MV infrastructure are available to commission the remaining containers. "Full Order Delay" — Delay caused by late delivery, defect, or unavailability of any Critical Path Equipment, resulting in no portion of the order being capable of commissioning. "Partial Delay" — Delay caused by late delivery, defect, or unavailability of Non-Critical Path Equipment, where the remaining equipment is capable of commissioning at reduced capacity. "Affected Capacity" — The proportion of total ordered energy capacity (MWh) that cannot be commissioned due to the delayed equipment, expressed as a percentage of total capacity. "LD Value" — For a Full Order Delay: the total order value (all goods). For a Partial Delay: the value of the delayed goods only (= order value × Affected Capacity %). 9C.1 Delay Liquidated Damages: (a) Days 1–30 of delay: 0.1% of LD Value per day; (b) Days 31–60 of delay: 0.15% of LD Value per day; (c) Days 61+ of delay: 0.2% of LD Value per day; (d) Cap: aggregate delay LDs shall not exceed 10% of LD Value per order. 9C.2 Application: (a) PCS or MV Skid delayed → Full Order Delay → LD Value = total order value. (b) Individual battery container delayed (others can commission) → Partial Delay → LD Value = value of delayed container(s) only. (c) Delay LDs begin on the day following the agreed delivery date and accrue daily until actual delivery or termination. 9C.3 Exclusions: Delay LDs shall not apply where the delay is caused by: (a) Buyer's failure to pay on time; (b) Force Majeure under Section 15; (c) Buyer's failure to provide site readiness as required.

Comment 4: INSERT — Performance Guarantees & SOH CRITICAL MISSING SECTION

Location in New Contract: Not present — should be inserted AFTER Article 8C (LTSA Separation) as Section 8D

Issue: The contract contains no binding performance specifications — no State of Health (SOH) guarantees, no Round-Trip Efficiency (RTE) commitment, no availability target. While §8C correctly separates LTSA-governed guarantees, the OEM product specifications (SOH, RTE, cycle life) should be contractually warranted as product characteristics, independent of any LTSA.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST Section 8D was drafted in linyang-sales-comments.html with full RFI-confirmed specifications. All values confirmed by Linyang in RFI V1–V3 (February 2026).

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: While availability LDs and service-level commitments properly belong in the LTSA, the product's inherent performance characteristics (SOH, RTE, cycle life) are manufacturer warranties on the product itself and must be warranted in this Sales Contract. Without these, Lighthief has no contractual basis to hold Linyang accountable for product performance.

Proposed New Section — Insert as "Section 8D — PRODUCT PERFORMANCE WARRANTIES":

8D.1 State of Health (SOH) Guarantees: The Seller warrants that the battery cells and modules, when operated in accordance with the Seller's guidelines (1 cycle/day average, 90% DoD, 25±2°C), shall retain the following minimum State of Health: (a) Year 5: ≥ 85% SOH; (b) Year 10: ≥ 79.58% SOH; (c) Year 15: ≥ 70% SOH. SOH remedy: The Seller shall supply replacement cells/modules (FOB or CIF Limassol as applicable) to restore capacity above the guaranteed threshold. Replacement parts are at Seller's cost; installation labour is at Buyer's cost unless an LTSA provides otherwise. 8D.2 Round-Trip Efficiency (RTE): The Seller warrants that the full system AC-AC Round-Trip Efficiency, including PCS conversion, auxiliary consumption, and internal DC/AC cabling losses within the Seller's supply scope, shall be not less than 86.32% at rated power under standard test conditions (0.5C charge / 0.5C discharge, 25±2°C). This warranty is conditional upon the Buyer's external MV cabling (from MV Skid to Point of Common Coupling) conforming to the cable specifications and maximum allowable loss parameters set out in the Seller's RTE calculation documents and system design drawings. The Seller shall provide the Buyer with the applicable cable loss budget and specifications within fourteen (14) days of contract signing. Any RTE shortfall attributable solely to Buyer-supplied cables exceeding the specified loss ratings shall not constitute a breach of this warranty. 8D.3 Cycle Life: The Seller warrants a minimum cycle life of 7,000 equivalent full cycles at a standard charge/discharge rate of 0.5C/0.5C, at 90% Depth of Discharge (DoD) to 70% End of Life (EOL), at 25±2°C, under the operating conditions specified in the Seller's Warranty Terms (Version 2, as referenced in the Linyang RFI Compliance Confirmation, February 2026). For the avoidance of doubt, the maximum charge/discharge rate is 1C. Operation at rates exceeding 0.5C may affect achievable cycle life and shall be governed by the degradation curves provided in the Seller's Warranty Terms V2. 8D.4 PCS Efficiency: The Seller warrants PCS conversion efficiency of not less than 98% at rated power. 8D.5 Remedies: If the Products fail to meet the performance thresholds in §8D.1–8D.4, the Seller's obligation shall be limited to repair or replacement of defective components in accordance with §8 (Warranty). For the avoidance of doubt, availability LDs and revenue-based compensation remain governed exclusively by any applicable LTSA under §8C. 8D.6 Reference Documents: The following Seller documents, as confirmed in the Linyang RFI Compliance Response (February 2026), form part of the performance warranty and are incorporated by reference: (a) Warranty Terms — Version 2; (b) Battery Container Specification (3.34 MWh); (c) PCS Datasheets (BCS-C-HUD series); (d) RTE Calculation Report (including cable loss budget assumptions).

The discrepancy arises because system-level cycle life accounts for auxiliary consumption and a stricter EOL threshold. IS THE WARRANTY DOCUMENT SUFFICIENT ON ITS OWN?

The Warranty Terms V2 (confirmed in Linyang's RFI Compliance Response) contains the detailed SOH curves, cycle life methodology, and degradation parameters. However, relying on the warranty document alone presents risks:
  • Risk 1: The warranty document is a unilateral Seller document — Linyang can revise it without contractual consent. If the key thresholds (SOH 85/79.58/70%, RTE 86.32%, 7,000 cycles at 0.5C) are stated in the contract body, they become bilaterally agreed and cannot be unilaterally changed.
  • Risk 2: The warranty document may use qualifying language ("typical," "expected," "under ideal conditions") that weakens enforceability. The contract should use "warrants" or "guarantees."
  • Risk 3: Dispute resolution — if a performance claim goes to arbitration, having the thresholds in the contract makes them express terms with clear remedies, rather than implied terms from a referenced document.
Recommendation: State the headline thresholds in the contract (§8D) AND incorporate the Warranty Terms V2 by reference for detailed methodology, degradation curves, and testing protocols. This gives both legal certainty and technical depth.
If Linyang refuses to include performance warranties in the contract body:
  • Option A (Preferred): Include the SOH, RTE, and cycle life specifications in the Quotation/Purchase Order as binding product specifications — reference them as "warranted specifications" rather than "indicative."
  • Option B: Add an Annex to this contract titled "Product Specifications and Warranted Performance" incorporating the Warranty Terms V2 by reference, with a clause stating: "The performance parameters in the Warranty Terms V2 constitute binding product warranties. Any inconsistency between this Contract and the Warranty Terms shall be resolved in favour of the higher performance obligation."
  • Option C: Rely on the LTSA as the enforcement mechanism (current §8C approach), but this only works if every end-customer has an LTSA — which may not always be the case, and it does not give Lighthief direct recourse against Linyang.
  • Option D: Require Linyang to provide a separate Product Performance Guarantee Letter referencing the RFI confirmed values, attached as a side letter.
ACTION REQUIRED — LINYANG WARRANTY TERMS V2:
Request Linyang to provide the final Warranty Terms V2 document for Lighthief's review prior to contract execution. If the warranty document specifies different conditions (e.g., 80% EOL vs. 70% EOL, or different temperature ranges), the contract must clarify which values prevail.

Comment 5: Liability Cap — Restore to 100% CRITICAL

Location in New Contract: Article 9 — Limitation of Liability, penultimate paragraph

Issue: The general liability cap has been reduced from 100% of total price paid (our amended version §13.2) to 10% of total charges. This is a 90% reduction in Lighthief's maximum recovery in the event of a major breach. On a ~€85M contract, this limits recovery to ~€8.5M regardless of actual damage.

Important distinction: The 10% cap confirmed in the RFI relates specifically to the Total Combined LD Cap (delay + availability LDs aggregated) — NOT the general liability cap for all breaches. The new contract conflates these two concepts. The RFI confirmed 10% as the LD cap; the general liability cap was set at 100% in our amended agreement.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST Our amended sales agreement §13.2 explicitly set the general cap at 100%. The 10% warranty-specific cap (§12.11) was agreed separately for warranty claims only, with a manufacturing defect carve-out. The 10% total LD cap (§9C.4) was RFI-confirmed. These are three distinct caps — the new contract has collapsed them into one, which is unacceptable.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: We accept the 10% cap for warranty services (Article 8B — already in the contract). However, the general liability cap covering all breaches must be restored to 100% of the total price paid. A 10% general cap is commercially inadequate for a contract of this value and does not reflect the agreed position.

Current Text (New Contract §9):

Subject to other clauses in this section, the total liability of the SELLER of all breaches of duty under this Contract shall not in any event or under any circumstances, exceed ten percent of total charges.

Proposed Replacement Text:

Subject to other clauses in this section, the total aggregate liability of the SELLER for all breaches of duty under this Contract shall not exceed one hundred percent (100%) of the total price paid by the BUYER to the SELLER for the Products giving rise to the claim. For the avoidance of doubt, this general liability cap is without prejudice to the warranty-specific liability cap in Section 8B, which shall apply to warranty claims independently.
If Linyang insists on a cap below 100%:
  • Compromise A: Accept a 50% general liability cap as a middle ground — still significantly better than 10%.
  • Compromise B: Accept tiered caps: 10% for warranty claims (already agreed), 50% for general contractual breaches, 100% for fraud/wilful misconduct and manufacturing defect indemnities.
  • Compromise C: Accept 10% general cap but add explicit carve-outs for: (i) delay LDs under §9C, (ii) indemnities under §9A, (iii) APG/Performance Bond obligations, (iv) IP infringement, (v) fraud/wilful misconduct. This effectively preserves the most important protections even under a low cap.

Comment 8: Late Payment Interest Rate IMPORTANT

Location in New Contract: Article 6 — Terms of Payment, sub-clause on Cyprus interest rate

Issue: The contract specifies 2% per month, compounded monthly for Cyprus — equating to 26.8% APR. This is commercially punitive and may be unenforceable under Cyprus usury law. Our amended version uses the "maximum statutory interest rate" which is more balanced and legally defensible.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: The 2% monthly compound interest rate is excessive. Please replace with a commercially reasonable rate aligned with statutory limits.

Current Text:

For contracts where the end destination is Cyprus, the interest rate shall be 2% per month, compounded monthly, as per Cyprus commercial practice.

Proposed Replacement Text:

For contracts where the end destination is Cyprus, the interest rate on late payments shall be the European Central Bank (ECB) main refinancing rate plus three percent (3%) per annum, calculated on a simple interest basis, or the maximum rate permitted under applicable Cyprus law, whichever is lower.
If Linyang insists on a fixed rate: Propose 1% per month, simple interest (not compounded) — this is 12% APR, which is commercially firm but not punitive. Alternatively, accept 2% but remove compounding (simple interest only) and add a cap of 5% of the overdue amount.

Comment 9: Confidentiality — Correct Governing Law Reference IMPORTANT

Location in New Contract: Article 16 — Confidentiality Clause, final sentence

Issue: The confidentiality clause states it is governed by "the applicable laws of the Republic of Poland" — this is a template remnant. The contract's governing law is Singapore (§12.1), and Lighthief is a Cyprus entity. Poland has no connection to this contract.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: Please correct the governing law reference in the confidentiality clause from "Republic of Poland" to align with the contract's governing law (Singapore) or the Buyer's jurisdiction (Republic of Cyprus).

Current Text:

This confidentiality clause is permanent unless the confidential information is made public or disclosed in accordance with the applicable laws of the Republic of Poland..

Proposed Replacement Text:

This confidentiality clause shall survive termination of the Contract for a period of five (5) years, unless the confidential information enters the public domain through no fault of the receiving Party or is required to be disclosed under applicable law or regulation, including the laws of the Republic of Cyprus and Singapore.
Note: This is likely an oversight from adapting the Polish Solarfun template. Linyang should accept this correction without objection, as the current text is legally inconsistent with the rest of the contract.

Comment 10: Non-Conforming Goods — Return at Seller's Expense IMPORTANT

Location in New Contract: Article 5 — Delivery and Inspection, §5.6

Issue: The contract requires the Buyer to return non-conforming goods at Buyer's expense within 5 days of Seller's receipt of notice. This is unfair — if the Seller delivered defective goods, the cost of return should fall on the Seller.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: Where non-conformance is confirmed, the return of defective Commodities should be at Seller's expense. Lighthief should not bear logistics costs for Seller's defective products.

Current Text:

BUYER shall ship, at its expense and risk of loss, the nonconforming Commodities to SELLER's facility within [five] days of SELLER's receipt of Buyer's written notice

Proposed Replacement Text:

BUYER shall ship the nonconforming Commodities to the SELLER's facility within ten (10) Business Days of the SELLER's confirmation that the claim is accepted. Shipping costs and risk of loss for confirmed non-conforming Commodities shall be borne by the SELLER. Where the SELLER disputes the non-conformance, the Parties shall follow the dispute procedure in §12.12 (Warranty Dispute Resolution) and shipping costs shall be borne by the Party determined to be at fault.
If Linyang insists Buyer bears initial shipping costs: Accept, but add: "The Seller shall reimburse the Buyer's reasonable shipping costs within thirty (30) days where the non-conformance is confirmed as a manufacturing defect."

Comment 11: INSERT — Product Compliance Standards IMPORTANT

Location in New Contract: Article 20 — Compliance with Laws, after §20.3

Issue: The current compliance section (§20) covers only general anti-bribery and sanctions. It does not require the Products to meet specific technical standards (IEC, CE, Cyprus grid codes). Our amended version (§20.4–20.5) includes explicit product compliance requirements.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST Section 20.4–20.5 in our amended sales agreement.

20.4 Product Standards Compliance: The Products supplied under this Contract shall comply with: (a) IEC standards applicable to battery energy storage systems (including IEC 62619, IEC 62477); (b) CE marking requirements for products placed on the European Union/EEA market; (c) applicable Cyprus Distribution System Operator (DSO) grid codes and technical regulations; and (d) any additional standards specified in the applicable Quotation. 20.5 Certificates of Compliance: Upon request, the Seller shall provide certificates of compliance, test reports, and documentation evidencing compliance with applicable standards, including but not limited to CE certificates, IEC test reports, and type approval documentation.

Comment 12: INSERT — No Set-Off for LTSA Matters MINOR

Issue: The contract separates LTSA obligations (§8C) but does not explicitly prevent set-off. Without this, either party could attempt to withhold payments under this Sales Contract citing LTSA or EMS disputes.

Previously Requested: PRIOR REQUEST Section 11.5 in our amended sales agreement.

LIGHTHIEF COMMENT: For clarity and to support the LTSA separation principle in §8C, please add a no set-off provision.

8C.2 No Set-Off: The Buyer shall not withhold, defer, or set off payments under this Contract due to performance or service matters governed by an LTSA or by third-party EMS arrangements.
