  • Buyer (Maintenance Provider): Performs all preventive & corrective maintenance, monitoring, and on-site service using the Buyer's own service team and at the Buyer's own cost. Manages and pays for EMS operations and licensing via contracted provider (currently Voltus Energy). All maintenance and operational costs are funded from LTSA service fees paid by Park Owners to the Buyer.
  • Seller (OEM Guarantee & Support): Backs SOH guarantees (§B2), supplies warranty parts (§12 & §B2.4), provides remote diagnostics, specialist technicians, and firmware updates.
  • No hands-on maintenance by Seller: Routine maintenance is performed by the Buyer's qualified personnel per the OEM manual.

B4.3 No Separate Annual OEM Support Fee:

The Seller's OEM guarantee support obligations under this Agreement — including SOH guarantee backing (Section B2), warranty replacement parts supply (Sections 12 and B2.4), remote diagnostic support, specialist OEM technician availability, and firmware updates and patches — are included as part of the Seller's binding commitments under Part B and are covered by the Product purchase price. No separate annual OEM support fee is payable by the Buyer to the Seller.

The Buyer bears its own costs for: (a) the service team performing preventive and corrective maintenance under downstream LTSAs; (b) EMS operations and licensing (currently contracted via Voltus Energy); and (c) all on-site labour, logistics, and spare parts installation. These costs are funded from the LTSA service fees paid by Park Owners to the Buyer and are retained entirely by the Buyer.

The Seller's only annual payment entitlement from the Buyer, beyond the Product purchase price, is the Extended Warranty fee under Section B3 (if and when exercised by the Buyer).

PART C — BACK-TO-BACK SERVICE FRAMEWORKGoverning the Seller's Obligations in Respect of Buyer's Downstream Commitments
Purpose: This Part ensures that where the Buyer incurs liability to end customers due to equipment issues within the Seller's responsibility, the Seller stands behind its Part B commitments through a clear indemnity and remedy chain.

C1. Transparency and Acknowledgement

C1.1 The Seller acknowledges that the Buyer enters into EPC Agreements and LTSAs with end customers. Material downstream terms are in Exhibit 1.

C1.2 The Seller confirms it has reviewed Exhibit 1 and accepts that the Buyer's downstream commitments are derived from and consistent with the Seller's Part B commitments.

C1.3 No direct privity between Seller and end customers. Buyer remains the contracting party.

C1.4 Material changes to downstream terms beyond Exhibit 1 require Seller's written consent for Part C to apply.

C2. Equipment-Related LD Indemnity

Where Buyer pays client LDs and the root cause is within Seller's responsibility, Seller reimburses Buyer.

Indemnity Triggers (§C2.1)

  • Manufacturing defect in the Products
  • Design defect in the Products
  • Equipment underperformance within Seller's warranty scope
  • Failure to meet Part B performance guarantees
  • Failure to deliver replacement components within Part B timeframes

Process (§C2.2)

  • Buyer notifies Seller within 14 days of becoming aware
  • Joint investigation — root cause determination within 30 days
  • If disputed: independent third-party testing (loser pays)

Exclusions (§C2.4)

  • Buyer's EPC works (civil, cabling, grounding)
  • End customer misuse or operational non-compliance
  • EMS or third-party system failures
  • Force Majeure, grid unavailability, curtailment
  • Buyer's failure to perform OEM-specified maintenance
  • Downstream terms exceeding Part B without Seller consent

Indemnity Cap (§C2.3): Total price paid by Buyer for that project's Products (consistent with §13.2).

C3. SOH Shortfall Remedy Chain

StepActionTimeline
1. NotificationBuyer notifies Seller of SOH shortfall with test resultsWithin 14 days
2. VerificationSeller may request verification test (OEM protocol)Within 30 days of notification
3. RemedySeller supplies replacement cells/modules CIF LimassolWithin 90 days of verified shortfall
4. InstallationBuyer installs on-site (included in LTSA fees)Per Buyer's schedule
Seller Default (§C3.4): If Seller fails to deliver within 90 days, Buyer may source alternatives. Seller reimburses up to 120% of equivalent component pricing plus any downstream LDs caused by the delay (subject to §C2.3 cap).

C4. Extended Warranty Exercise

C4.1 Buyer exercises by written notice. Seller acknowledges within 14 days.

C4.2 Unconditional obligation: Seller must honour at Part B3 rates. Same scope as base warranty including Manufacturing Defect Carve-Out.

C4.3 Failure to honour: Buyer may source alternative coverage. Seller reimburses incremental cost above Part B3 rates (subject to §13.2 cap).

C5. Availability — Responsibility Allocation

C5.1 Buyer's Availability Commitment: The Buyer offers a 97% annual availability guarantee to end customers under Tier C LTSAs. The Buyer is responsible for achieving this target through its own preventive maintenance programme, EMS management, and on-site service capabilities.

C5.2 Seller's Support Obligations:

  • Maintaining spare parts supply capability
  • Remote OEM diagnostic support within 24 hours
  • Specialist OEM technicians when Buyer's team cannot resolve
  • Firmware updates and patches for known issues
  • Warranty replacement parts within 45 days of validated claim

C5.3 Fault Attribution:

  • Product defect → Seller's responsibility: Manufacturing/design defects, OEM firmware issues, equipment underperformance within warranty — excluded from Buyer's maintenance availability calculations.
  • Maintenance failure → Buyer's responsibility: Failure to perform maintenance, delayed response, EMS failures, third-party issues.
  • Disputes: Resolved by mutual BMS/EMS data log review; failing agreement, by independent expert under §14.

C5.4 Maintenance Allowance: 10 days/year (240 hours) scheduled downtime. Seller coordinates any OEM-required maintenance within this allowance.
