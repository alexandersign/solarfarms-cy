ANALYSIS — FEBRUARY 2026

Why Cyprus Is Falling Behind the EU on Battery Storage — And What It's Costing Your Solar Park

If you own a solar park in Cyprus, you already know the numbers are getting worse. Curtailment in 2025 reached 47% — nearly half of all distributed solar generation was wasted. A 5 MW park that should generate 8,500 MWh/year is now producing barely 4,500 MWh of revenue-generating output. At current PPA rates, that's €200,000–€580,000 per year evaporating.

But here's what most park owners don't know: this problem is almost entirely unique to Cyprus in the EU. Every other European country has either solved it or is solving it faster. Here's the evidence.

What the 50% Discharge Cap Actually Costs You

This is the restriction most park owners don't understand, and it's the most expensive. Let's use real TSOC Day-Ahead Market data from October 2025 – February 2026 (134 days of actual market clearing prices).

Peak hours (17:00–21:00) average price: €182.99/MWh
Midday hours (10:00–14:00) average price: €101.13/MWh
Price at 19:00 (daily high): €186/MWh
Price at 12:00 (daily low): €77/MWh

Take a 10 MW PV + 10 MW / 40 MWh BESS (4-hour duration, Category B):

ScenarioDischarge PowerDischarge WindowRevenue/Cycle

Over a year (~330 cycles), that's €240,000–€330,000 lost annually on a single 10 MW installation. Over 15 years: €3.6–€5.0 million in foregone revenue.

The cap forces you to start discharging at 13:00 when prices are €105/MWh instead of concentrating output at 17:00–21:00 when prices are €183/MWh. You're selling into the solar trough — the exact hours the battery was meant to avoid.

The Must-Run Problem: Why Curtailment Exists

TSOC mandates 210–250 MW of conventional thermal generation running at all times for "grid inertia." This means even when solar could power the entire island, fossil fuel plants keep running and solar gets curtailed to make room.

The cost to consumers: €250–350 million per year in EU carbon allowance (ETS) costs, passed directly through electricity bills. The cumulative ETS bill from 2018–2025: approximately €1.28 billion.

How have other countries solved this?

  • Ireland — Reduced must-run thermal from ~50% to ~20% using BESS for inertia (DS3 programme, operational since 2016)
  • Germany — Launched a dedicated inertia services market in January 2026, paying BESS €8,000–17,000/MW/year
  • UK — 12.5+ GW of BESS deployed through the Stability Pathfinder programme

Cyprus has done none of this. There is no inertia services market. BESS cannot compete with thermal plants for grid stability contracts. The result: a self-reinforcing cycle where solar is curtailed to protect fossil generation, and BESS is blocked from providing the alternative.

GUIDE — FEBRUARY 2026

How to Choose a BESS Partner: 10 Questions Every Solar Park Owner Must Ask Before Signing

Choosing the wrong battery storage partner can turn a profitable asset into a 15-year headache. The BESS market in Cyprus is new — most park owners have never procured a battery system before. Unlike solar panels (where EPC quality varies but the technology is commoditised), BESS systems have critical differences in cell chemistry, warranty terms, integration quality, and ongoing service that dramatically affect your returns.

Here are the 10 questions you must ask — and the answers that separate serious partners from box-sellers.

1. Who manufactured the cells, and are they Tier-1?

The cells are the heart of the system. Ask for the cell manufacturer, model, and whether they are rated by independent agencies (BNEF Tier 1, DNV, TÜV). Cells from unknown or white-label manufacturers carry significantly higher failure risk.

What good looks like: Named Tier-1 cell manufacturer (CATL, BYD, EVE, CALB), with UL 9540A fire safety certification, and independently verified cycle life data.

Lighthief uses: EVE 314Ah LFP cells (via Linyang Energy) — Tier-1 rated, UL 9540A certified, 7,000 cycles at 90% DoD guaranteed to 70% EOL (Linyang RFI confirmed Feb 2026).

2. What is the actual round-trip efficiency (AC-to-AC)?

Many suppliers quote DC-to-DC efficiency (which ignores inverter and transformer losses). Always ask for AC-to-AC round-trip efficiency — this is what matters for revenue. The difference between 85% and 90% RTE is significant over 15 years.

What good looks like: ≥86% AC-to-AC RTE, tested under operating conditions, with contractual guarantee.

Lighthief guarantees: 86.32% AC-to-AC RTE (full system incl. cabling), contractually guaranteed, verified by Linyang factory testing.

3. What exactly does the warranty cover — and what doesn't it?

This is where most buyers get burned. A "5-year warranty" can mean very different things:

  • Product warranty = manufacturing defects only (limited)
  • Performance warranty = guaranteed SOH and RTE over time (comprehensive)
  • Capacity guarantee = specific MWh capacity at defined year milestones

Ask for the warranty document. Read the exclusions. Understand what triggers a claim and what the remedy is (repair, replace, or cash compensation).

What good looks like: 5-year base product + performance warranty, with optional LTSA extension to 10–15 years, including SOH milestones (e.g., ≥85% at Year 5, ≥79.6% at Year 10, ≥70% at Year 15).

Lighthief offers: 5-year base warranty + LTSA extensions up to 15 years. Guaranteed SOH: ≥85% Year 5, ≥79.6% Year 10, ≥70% Year 15. Extended warranty pricing: Years 6–10 at €913/MWh/yr (BESS) + €747/MWh (PCS).

4. Who provides O&M, and what's included?

BESS systems need active maintenance — thermal management, cell balancing, firmware updates, fire suppression checks. The cheapest system is worthless if the O&M provider is unreachable or unqualified.

What good looks like: Single-source O&M from the EPC contractor, with 24/7 remote monitoring, guaranteed response times, and a fixed annual fee that includes all routine maintenance.

Lighthief O&M: €1,740/MWh/year all-inclusive (BESS + PCS + monitoring). Same team that built it maintains it. No third-party handoffs. Optional 97% availability guarantee at €2,201/MWh/year.

5. Is the EPC contractor also the O&M provider?

When the company that designed and built the system also maintains it, there's no finger-pointing. If a cell fails, they can't blame the installer. If the PCS underperforms, they can't blame the manufacturer. Single-source accountability is worth more than any discount from splitting contracts.

Lighthief: Single partner for EPC + O&M. We design, procure, build, commission, and maintain. One contract, one counterparty, one phone number.

Some suppliers quote CIF equipment cost (which excludes installation, civil works, grid connection, commissioning, and testing). Always compare total installed cost per MWh (sometimes called "all-in EPC cost").

9. What happens when DAM arbitrage legislation passes?

Cyprus is the only EU country where BESS cannot buy from the Day-Ahead Market. When this changes (and it will — EU Directive 2019/944 requires it), your BESS will unlock additional revenue of approximately €71.87/MWh per cycle in grid arbitrage.

Ask your BESS partner: "Is the system designed to support grid charging when legislation changes? Will the connection infrastructure allow reclassification from Category B to Category C?"

Lighthief designs for the future: All our installations are designed with connection infrastructure that supports future reclassification to Category C (grid-charging capable). When the law changes, your system is ready — no additional hardware cost.
