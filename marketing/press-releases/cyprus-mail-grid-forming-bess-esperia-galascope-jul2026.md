# Cyprus is finally building batteries — on parks that already waste sunshine

**Cyprus Mail · Guest Column · Energy**  
**By Alexander Papacosta** · Cyprus Director, Lighthief Cyprus Ltd · July 2026

*What EMS and BMS actually do, why grid-forming matters, how peak shaving works, what September’s schemes mean — and why solar ROI now lives in the hybrid era.*

---

On a clear spring midday in Cyprus, the sun does its job and the grid often does not. Solar parks generate clean electricity — then get told to switch off. Rooftop owners watch their inverters drop offline. Evening comes, demand rises, and the island burns imported fuel again. Everyone pays for that waste somehow: on the bill, in the carbon cost, or in the quiet frustration of watching free energy thrown away.

That story is familiar. What is new is that the metal is starting to arrive — and with it, a vocabulary that used to live only in engineering rooms. If you are going to live through Cyprus’s storage decade, it helps to know what the boxes actually do.

## The quieter deal: batteries where the waste happens

This summer the headlines belonged to the big public projects — 120 MW of Transmission System Operator batteries, the Electricity Authority’s Dhekelia system, the minister’s promise of “several hundred megawatts” by the end of 2027. An isolated island needs a backbone. But backbone alone will not stop every park from spilling sunshine at noon. You also need batteries *on the parks themselves*.

That is the quieter story. Eleven parks. Roughly 80 MW of solar. More than 300 MWh of storage. Not a future licence map. Parks that already produce, already curtail, and will now have somewhere to put the surplus instead of dumping it.

We are putting batteries next to that problem — with Linyang’s equipment and a Cyprus team that has to make them work for twenty years, not for a ribbon-cutting.”

## BMS vs EMS — the nurse and the air-traffic controller

Every serious battery system has two brains. Confusing them is how buyers get sold the wrong thing.

**BMS — Battery Management System**  
Lives inside the container. Watches every cell’s voltage and temperature, balances packs, calculates state of charge and health, and trips the system before it cooks itself. Think of it as the nurse on the ward: vitals, alarms, safety first.

**EMS — Energy Management System**  
Sits above the hardware and decides *what the battery should do for the business and the grid*: charge now, discharge at 18:00, hold reserve, talk to the DSO’s SCADA. Think of it as air-traffic control: timing, money, and network rules.

A brilliant BMS with a dumb EMS is a safe brick. A clever EMS without a trustworthy BMS is a liability. Cyprus Category B hybrids — co-located solar plus storage — need both: Linyang’s BMS for the chemistry, and an EMS that can take EAC commands, protect warranties, and chase the evening peak without cooking the cells.

## Grid-following vs grid-forming — passenger or conductor

Most people hear “battery” and picture something that stores electricity. Fair enough. On an island with no cable to anywhere, the more interesting question is whether the inverter can help keep the grid steady.

**Grid-following** locks onto voltage and frequency that something else is already creating — usually a conventional power station — and injects or absorbs current. Excellent passenger. Needs the orchestra playing.

**Grid-forming** can create its own voltage and frequency reference. In virtual synchronous generator mode it behaves more like a spinning machine: synthetic inertia, black-start potential, the ability to steady a weak island grid. Can be the conductor.

On the systems we bring through Linyang, that difference is largely in the power conversion firmware — not a different shipping container. Plants can connect in following mode first, then switch to forming when the DSO and TSO frameworks allow it. In March we set out in these pages why that matters for Cyprus’s expensive “must-run” habit. The short version: storage that only shifts kilowatt-hours is useful; storage that can compete with oil-fired machines for stability is transformative.

> “Cyprus has spent years explaining why it cannot use all its solar. The next chapter is simpler: put storage where the waste happens, and let that storage earn its keep on the grid. Public batteries matter. Private ones on real parks matter too — because that is where the sunshine is actually being thrown away.”
>
> — **Dr Arkadius Sybaris**, Founder & CEO, Lighthief

## Peak shaving — the unglamorous job that pays bills

Peak shaving is the least romantic phrase in energy — and one of the most useful.

For a **solar park**, the Cyprus version looks like this: at midday the DSO says stop exporting; the battery charges from panels that would otherwise be curtailed (charge cost: roughly zero); between about 17:00 and 21:00, when Cypriots come home and prices spike, the battery discharges. Day-ahead market data has shown a stubborn pattern — midday wholesale prices around €100 per megawatt-hour, evening peaks nearer €180–200. That spread is why co-located storage is not charity. It is recovering energy already paid for in panels and land.

For a **hotel, warehouse or factory**, peak shaving means something else: cut the expensive spikes on your EAC bill. Charge from your rooftop (or off-peak where allowed), discharge into your own loads when demand peaks, and stop buying the most painful kilowatt-hours from the grid. Same battery physics. Different cash register.

## September’s schemes — money catching up with metal

Hardware without policy is a parked Ferrari. Policy without hardware is a press conference. This autumn, the two start overlapping more visibly.

- **Save-Upgrade Houses** — the energy ministry has said the next home energy-efficiency funding round opens in **September**, with a €20 million budget and grants of up to €32,000 per house for upgrades that can include photovoltaics, insulation, heating and cooling. It is not a pure “battery only” cheque — but it is another push for households to stop treating the roof as decoration.
- **Co-located storage support** — Cyprus has already launched a competitive €35 million scheme aimed at adding hundreds of megawatt-hours of storage beside existing renewable plants. That is the public twin of what private programmes like Esperia–Galascope are doing with their own capital.
- **Net billing, not net metering** — new solar connections increasingly live in a world where exported midday power is worth less than self-consumed evening power. Batteries stop being optional accessories and become the difference between a pretty roof and a working energy asset.

If you are a homeowner waiting for “the perfect scheme,” read the fine print when September opens — and ask whether a battery is in scope for *your* tariff and building age. If you are a park owner, do not wait for a grant to invent the physics. The curtailment is already on your invoice.

## Solar ROI in the hybrid era

Ten years ago, a Cyprus solar pitch was simple: panels, sun, sell. That model cracked when curtailment went from curiosity to crisis — from near-zero a few years ago toward the mid-forties per cent on hard days. Solar-only returns got thinner precisely when the island needed clean power most.

The hybrid era changes the spreadsheet. For co-located utility projects of the kind we model for park owners, indicative returns typically sit in the **8–13% annual** range with paybacks around **7–10 years**, depending on curtailment, tariff path and financing — with BESS recovering a meaningful share of energy that would otherwise be thrown away. For commercial rooftops chasing bill reduction rather than wholesale export, well-matched sites often see paybacks in the **low-to-mid single-digit years**, because every kilowatt-hour you do not buy from EAC is cash kept.

None of those numbers are a promise. They are a direction: in Cyprus today, solar without storage is a half-finished sentence. Solar *with* a BMS that keeps the chemistry honest, an EMS that times the discharges, and — when rules allow — grid-forming capability that helps the island grid, is how the asset earns its keep after the ribbon is cut.

## What changes for you

Not overnight, and not with a magic tariff cut next month. But the direction is concrete. More co-located storage means less wasted midday solar, more clean power in the evening peak, sharper commercial bills, and — if policymakers keep up — less need to keep expensive conventional plant warm purely for stability.

For park owners still bleeding energy every sunny weekend: stop waiting for the perfect market design before ordering the thing that stops the waste. For everyone else reading this over morning coffee: notice when storage is treated as a side project, and when it is treated as infrastructure that belongs next to the panels already baking in the Cypriot sun.
