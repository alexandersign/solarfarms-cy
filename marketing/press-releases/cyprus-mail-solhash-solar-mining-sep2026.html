Cyprus solar parks don’t have to sit idle — solar mining turns waiting into revenue

Lighthief EPC, mining partners’ hardware, park-owner revenue share — and data centres only where scale makes sense.

By Alexander Papacosta · Cyprus Director, Lighthief Cyprus Ltd · September 2026

Across Cyprus, megawatts of solar are already built or permitted while grid connection clocks tick for years. Panels bake in the sun. Inverters stay dark. Owners pay land, debt and opportunity cost for an asset that produces nothing until the DSO or TSO says go.

That wait used to be a dead zone. It does not have to be.

Solhash — Lighthief’s solar-mining programme — puts a simple three-party deal on those sites: the park owner provides the solar and the ground; a mining partner brings the miners; Lighthief delivers the EPC that makes the two work together as one plant. The park owner earns a revenue share from daytime generation that would otherwise be stranded. When connection terms finally arrive, the mining load can scale down or move — containers are mobile by design — and the park steps into its normal grid life, often with BESS already in the plan.

“Cyprus has parks waiting three to five years for a wire. That is not a technology problem — it is a cashflow problem. Solhash turns idle midday megawatts into paid work for the park owner, without asking them to become crypto operators overnight.” — Alexander Papacosta, Cyprus Director, Lighthief Cyprus Ltd

Why Lighthief is the natural partner

Solar mining fails when it is treated as a shipping container dumped next to a fence. It works when someone understands Cyprus PV, Cyprus heat, Category B hybrids, DSO reality, and battery behaviour in the same sentence.

That is Lighthief’s lane. We already EPC solar and BESS across the island — co-located storage, grid-forming PCS pathways, EMS/BMS integration, civil and electrical delivery. Mining containers are another controllable load on that same stack: LV distribution, cooling, Starlink or fibre, power limits that follow the solar curve, and — where BESS is present — bridging clouds so hashrate does not fall off a cliff every time a shadow passes.

A pure mining shop knows ASICs. A pure EPC shop knows cables. Cyprus needs both in one team. Our BESS expertise matters because the best sites are often the hybrid ones: solar that will later sell to the grid, storage that will later peak-shave, and a daytime mining load that monetises the years in between.

“Europe’s next industrial wave will be power-hungry and location-flexible — mining today, AI and data loads tomorrow. Cyprus has sun, idle parks, and an EPC culture that can build to EU standards. Lighthief’s job is to make that marriage bankable for park owners and for partners who bring the machines.” — Dr Arkadius Sybaris, Founder & CEO, Lighthief

How the split works

Keep the roles clean:

  • Park owner — solar park (operational or pre-connection), site access, and a share of mining revenue. No need to buy ASICs or run a pool.
  • Mining partner — miners, containers, and mining operations capital. They chase hashrate; we chase a safe plant.
  • Lighthief — EPC and integration: electrical design, installation, commissioning, interfaces to PV and (where applicable) BESS, and local delivery under Cyprus rules.

Revenue share aligns interests. When the sun is strong, everyone earns. When clouds cut output, dynamic power control throttles miners to the available watts instead of tripping the park. That is solar mining done as engineering, not as a garage experiment.

Open to more mining partners — and to park owners

We already work with mining capital that wants Cyprus sun behind the meter. We are open to additional mining partners who can fund and operate professional fleets — not hobby boards — and who accept that Cyprus heat, logistics and grid codes are non-negotiable.

We are equally keen to hear from park owners: pre-connection sites sitting dark; operational parks with heavy curtailment looking for a daytime offtaker; developers who want early EPC that doubles as mining-ready LV. If your park has real megawatts and a real timeline problem, that is a Solhash conversation.

Data centres — but only at scale

Bitcoin mining is the fast bridge: containers, mobile, daytime-tolerant. Data centre and GPU loads are the longer industrial play — higher value, stricter uptime, heavier design. We will discuss those options, but only for larger projects over 5 MW. Below that, the civil, cooling and connectivity overhead rarely clears the bar. Above it, a park can host a serious compute footprint with the same EPC and BESS discipline we already bring to hybrid solar.

Think of the ladder: curtailment recovery and solar mining first; hybrid BESS second; larger-than-5 MW data or AI loads where the site and partner deserve that complexity.

What Cyprus gets

Less wasted sunshine. Earlier cash for park owners stuck in the connection queue. A domestic use case for flexible load that does not wait for an interconnector. And a credible path from “idle panels” to “powered industry” without pretending every 1 MW plot is a hyperscale campus.

Solar mining will not replace the grid. It can stop Cyprus treating the wait for the grid as a write-off.
