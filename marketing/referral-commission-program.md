## 3. The Investment Case — Why BESS in Cyprus Now

### The Problem: Curtailment Is Destroying Solar Revenue

Cyprus PV park owners are losing a significant portion of their revenue to grid curtailment — energy that is generated but cannot be exported because the grid cannot absorb it.

| Year | Average Curtailment Rate | Impact |
|------|--------------------------|--------|
| 2021 | ~0% | No issue |
| 2022 | ~3.5% | Minimal |
| 2023 | ~13% | Noticeable losses |
| 2024 | ~29% | Significant revenue loss |
| 2025 | **~47%** | **Nearly half of solar output wasted** |

> A 5 MW solar park losing 40% of output at current market prices forfeits approximately **€400,000 — €500,000 per year** in revenue.

### The Solution: BESS Captures Lost Revenue

A Battery Energy Storage System stores curtailed solar energy during midday (when the grid rejects it) and discharges it during peak evening hours (when prices are highest).

**Current Cyprus Electricity Market Prices (verified Oct 2025 — Feb 2026):**

| Time Window | Average Market Price | BESS Action |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Midday (10:00 — 14:00) | ~€100/MWh | **Charge** from curtailed solar (cost: €0) |
| Peak Evening (17:00 — 21:00) | ~€183/MWh | **Discharge** and sell to grid |
| Spread | **~€83/MWh** | Per-cycle gross revenue |

### Three Revenue Streams

#### 1. Curtailment Recovery (Available Now)

The primary revenue driver today. BESS captures solar energy that would otherwise be wasted (curtailed) and sells it during peak hours.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Charge cost | **€0** (curtailed solar — would be wasted) |
| Discharge revenue | **~€183/MWh** (peak evening average) |
| System efficiency | **86.32%** AC-AC round-trip |
| Net revenue per cycle | **~€161/MWh discharged** |
| Daily cycles | 1 per day |

**Example — 10 MWh BESS:**
- Annual discharge revenue: **~€400,000 — €500,000**
- After O&M and degradation: **~€350,000 — €430,000 net**

#### 2. Energy Arbitrage (Future — Expected 2026-2027)

When Cyprus regulations allow BESS to charge from the grid (not just from co-located solar), operators can buy cheap midday energy and sell at peak prices.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Buy price (midday) | ~€100/MWh |
| Sell price (peak) | ~€183/MWh |
| Gross spread | **~€83/MWh per cycle** |
| Net after efficiency | **~€72/MWh per cycle** |

> Arbitrage adds a **second daily cycle** of revenue on top of curtailment recovery.

#### 3. Ancillary Services (Future — Grid Services Market)

As Cyprus develops its electricity market, BESS operators will be able to provide grid stability services for additional revenue.

| Service | Description | Expected Revenue |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| **Frequency Regulation (FCR)** | Sub-second response to frequency deviations | TBD — €20,000-50,000/MW/yr (EU benchmark) |
| **Secondary Reserve (aFRR)** | Automatic frequency restoration | TBD — comparable to EU markets |
| **Capacity Payments** | Availability-based compensation | TBD — under regulatory development |

> Ancillary services in mature EU markets (Germany, UK, Ireland) generate **€30,000 — €80,000 per MW per year** in additional revenue.
